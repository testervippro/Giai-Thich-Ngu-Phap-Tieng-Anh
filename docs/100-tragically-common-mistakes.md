## Chương 1: Lỗi ngữ pháp (Grammar Goofs)

### 1. Incomplete Sentences — Câu không hoàn chỉnh (câu cụt)

💡 **Giải thích:** Một câu hoàn chỉnh phải có **chủ ngữ** (người/vật thực hiện) và **vị ngữ** (nói gì về chủ ngữ). Những cụm bắt đầu bằng *whether, as if, because, when…* chỉ là **mệnh đề phụ**, phải gắn vào một mệnh đề chính đứng trước hoặc sau.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Whether the stolen shirt was in her purse. | The security guard wanted to see whether the stolen shirt was in her purse. |
| As if they hadn’t flirted already! | They acted as if they hadn't flirted already! |
| No way he could eat any more. | There was no way he could eat any more. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>It’s important to remember: “When a sentence is incomplete.” Um, what? When a sentence is incomplete, no one knows what you’re talking about. A half-finished sentence makes for a half-baked thought.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Most incomplete sentences are linked to a sentence either before or after the incomplete phrase in question. Adding “The security guard wanted to see” to “whether the stolen shirt was in her purse” transforms an incomplete sentence about a stolen shirt into a perfectly understandable complete sentence.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Every complete sentence needs a subject—the person, place, thing, or idea that is doing or being something—and a predicate (which finishes an idea about the subject).</p>

</details>

### 2. Gone and Went — Gone và Went

💡 **Giải thích:** *Go* là động từ bất quy tắc: **go – went – gone**. *Went* là **quá khứ đơn**, đứng một mình. *Gone* là **quá khứ phân từ**, luôn đi sau *have/has/had*. Không bao giờ nói *have went*. Mẹo: *He went to the store* (có thể vẫn đang ở đó), *He has gone home* (đã đi, không còn ở đây).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We’ve *went* to work every day this year. | We've gone to work every day this year. |
| Their cousin *gone* home. | Their cousin has gone home. / Their cousin went home. |
| My granddad said that he *gone* fishing. | My granddad said that he had gone fishing. / …that he went fishing. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p><em>Go</em> is one of the 200 or so irregular verbs that can make English-language learners tear out their hair. It’s simple enough to use “I go” or “he goes” correctly, but mastering the various tenses can be a struggle for even native English speakers.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The irregular verb <em>to go</em> uses simple past tense—<em>went</em> —to indicate something that may still be happening. Where is he? He <em>went</em> to the store. (Maybe he’s still there!) When you include a variant of the auxiliary verb <em>to have</em>, it changes into the perfect tense. Using <em>have</em> means the action is complete: Their cousin <em>has gone</em> home. (He isn’t returning!) You never <em>have went</em> anywhere, though.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Consider these sentences: “I <em>went</em> shopping” and “She <em>went</em> to school.” Are they permanent? They are not. At some point she’ll be coming home from school. No one writes a sad country song titled “She Went”; they all say that “she’s gone.” In the words of the late country singer Troy Gentry’s song “Gone,” when someone <em>has gone,</em> they are “like all the good things that ain’t never comin’ back.” Note that last phrase; it’s over.</p>

</details>

### 3. Phrasal Verbs — Cụm động từ (phrasal verbs)

💡 **Giải thích:** Hai lỗi phổ biến: (1) **đại từ đặt sai chỗ**: với cụm động từ tách được, đại từ (*it, them, him…*) phải nằm **giữa** động từ và tiểu từ (*divide them up*, không phải *divide up them*); (2) cụm ba từ kết thúc bằng giới từ như *look forward to* phải theo sau là **V-ing**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We sat down with the bagels and *divided up them*. | We sat down with the bagels and divided them up. |
| I’m looking forward to *see* you soon. | I'm looking forward to seeing you soon. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You use phrasal verbs all the time! Many are made up of two-word phrases with a verb and a preposition (<em>try out</em>) and some contain three words (<em>look forward to</em>). Even though they’re very common, it’s still easy to make a mistake when using them in a sentence.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The two most common errors are misplaced pronouns (as in <em>divided up them</em>), and neglecting to use an –ing verb in a three-word phrase. Keep the constituent parts of a phrasal verb together so that you <em>divide up</em> the bagels. In the common three-word phrases, don’t forget the –ing at the end of the verb. I’m looking forward to <em>seeing</em> you soon.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Some three-word phrasal verbs are transitive and need a noun to be complete. You can <em>get away with</em> murder, <em>look down on</em> hoi polloi, and <em>take care of</em> your aging relative.</p>

</details>

### 4. Who/Whom — Who và Whom

💡 **Giải thích:** Xem từ đó là **chủ ngữ** hay **tân ngữ**. Chủ ngữ → *who* (thay được bằng *he/she*). Tân ngữ (sau động từ, sau giới từ) → *whom* (thay được bằng *him/her*). Mẹo: trả lời thử: *He* → who; *him* → whom (cùng kết thúc bằng **m**).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Whom* does this belong to? | Whom does this belong to? → To whom does this belong? (trang trọng) / Who does this belong to? (văn nói) |
| Ask not for *who* the bell tolls. | Ask not for whom the bell tolls. |
| Let me know *whom* said that; I can’t remember *whom* it was. | Let me know who said that; I can't remember who it was. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>People seem to be terrified of <em>whom</em>, and for good reason. Most of us never learned how to use <em>whom</em> because—and this is a secret—our teachers didn’t understand it either. Fret no more! You may now use <em>whom</em> with confidence.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The heart of the matter is whether the word is the subject or object of the sentence. When you’re working with the subject, use who. <em>Who</em> said it? She said it! (As the subject of the sentence, you could also use her name instead of <em>who</em>. Hana said it!) If the word in question is the object of the sentence, use whom. This belongs to <em>whom</em>? It belongs to him.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Wondering if you’ve used the correct word? If you can replace the word with “he” or “she,” the proper choice is <em>who.</em> If the replacement can be “her” or “him,” <em>whom</em> is the correct usage.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>What order should <em>who</em> and <em>whom</em> appear in the following sentences? ---------- says I don’t know grammar? ---------- should I mark up with my red pen? He/she = who (subject), so “<em>who</em> says” is correct. Him/her = whom (object), so “<em>whom</em> should I” is correct. “<em>He</em> says” and “I should mark <em>him</em> up” answer the questions.</p>

</details>

### 5. Compound Relative Pronouns — Đại từ quan hệ ghép (whatever, whoever, wherever…)

💡 **Giải thích:** *Whatever, whichever, wherever, whoever, whomever* nối mệnh đề này với mệnh đề khác. Chọn đúng **nghĩa**: *wherever* = bất cứ nơi đâu (chỉ nơi chốn), *whoever* = bất cứ ai, *whichever* = bất cứ cái nào (trong một nhóm lựa chọn).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Go with those people, *whichever* they are headed. | Go with those people, wherever they are headed. |
| Did you tell them *whoever* you are? | Did you tell them who you are? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The next time a teenager rolls their eyes at you, says “Whatever!” and stomps away, lure them back with your grammatical brilliance by saying, “Oh, honey! You just correctly used a <em>compound relative pronoun</em>!” So many people are confused by this concept, but it’s easy to use the proper word.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Whatever</em>, <em>whichever</em>, <em>wherever</em>, <em>whoever</em>, and <em>whomever</em> are all compound relative pronouns. They join one clause to another, as in “<em>Whatever</em> candy you choose, I won’t be angry,” “Go with those people, <em>wherever</em> they are headed,” and “You may dance with <em>whomever</em> you wish.” These pronouns are inherently open and inviting of various options, which is, after all, what we all want in life.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>As pronouns, these words serve subjects or objects: “<em>Whoever</em> [subject] left their dishes on the table needs to return and clean up.” “I will go with <em>whomever</em> [object].” They are compounds because they add <em>–ever</em> to what, who, and other such question words: “<em>Whatever</em> do you want at this hour?” They also act as conjunctions because they join phrases: “I will eat <em>whatever</em> you’re eating.”</p>

</details>

### 6. Nominative/Objective/ Possessive — Chủ cách / tân cách / sở hữu cách của đại từ

💡 **Giải thích:** Chủ ngữ dùng *I, he, she, we, they*; tân ngữ (sau động từ, giới từ) dùng *me, him, her, us, them*; sở hữu dùng *my/mine, her/hers…* Lỗi hay gặp nhất là khi có **hai tân ngữ**: *to him and **me*** (không phải *him and I*). Mẹo: bỏ người kia đi, *bring that to I* nghe sai ngay.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Bring that to him and *I.* | Bring that to him and me. |
| *She* cats don’t belong to *my*. | Her cats don't belong to me. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Pronouns This common mistake is especially tricky when there are two pronouns. Once you learn how to use the proper pronoun, you’ll hear mistakes all around you. Luckily, there’s an easy trick to ensure you’ve chosen the right word.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The difficulty most often arises with more than one object pronoun. “Bring that to <em>him</em> and <em>me</em>” is right; many people will incorrectly say “<em>him</em> and <em>I</em>.” “I visited <em>him</em> and <em>her</em>” is correct, while “I visited <em>he</em> and <em>she</em>” is incorrect. To get it right every time, remove one of the two pronouns: “Bring that to <em>him</em>” (correct) and “Bring that to <em>I</em>” (incorrect). That second one sounds so wrong that you will correct yourself automatically: “Bring that to <em>him</em> and <em>me</em>.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Personal pronouns take three different forms: nominative (I), objective (me), and possessive (both my and mine, all mine). <em>I</em> am eating this chocolate (<em>I</em> am the subject). The chocolate belongs to <em>me</em> (the object is <em>me</em>). It is <em>mine</em> (a self-sufficient possessive pronoun). The subject pronouns include <em>I</em>, <em>you</em>, <em>he</em>, <em>she</em>, <em>it</em>, <em>we</em>, <em>you</em>, and <em>they</em>. Object pronouns include <em>me</em>, <em>you</em>, <em>him</em>, <em>her</em>, <em>it</em>, <em>us</em>, <em>you</em>, and <em>them</em>. Possessive pronouns include the dependent category of <em>my</em>, <em>your</em>, <em>his</em>, <em>her</em>, <em>its</em>, <em>our</em>, <em>your</em>, and <em>their</em>; they each take an object, as in my dog. The independent category includes <em>mine</em>, <em>yours</em>, <em>his</em>, <em>hers</em>, <em>its</em>, <em>ours</em>, <em>yours</em>, and <em>theirs</em>. Notice that none of the possessive pronouns use apostrophes.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which pronouns would you use, and in what order? ---------- sat there with ----------; ---------- didn’t realize that this table was ----------. Choose your favorite pronouns from the lists above! [Subject pronoun] sat there with [object pronoun]; [plural subject pronoun] didn’t realize that this table was [possessive pronoun]. For example, <em>I</em> sat there with <em>him</em>; <em>we</em> didn’t realize that this table was <em>hers</em>.</p>

</details>

### 7. To/Too/Two — To / Too / Two

💡 **Giải thích:** Ba từ phát âm giống nhau: **to** = giới từ (đến, để) hoặc *to + V*; **too** = quá, cũng; **two** = số 2.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| You’re *to* funny. | You're too funny. |
| We were going *two* buy some more popcorn today. | We were going to buy some more popcorn today. |
| They like cake so they bought *too* of them. | They like cake, so they bought two of them. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The main reason we confuse these three words is because they are homophones. They sound the same, so our overloaded minds mash them together into one word: <em>to</em>. It makes grammarians want to scream—two times!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Everyone uses these words correctly in speech because they sound the same. The preposition <em>to</em> is used to indicate that one is moving “for the purpose of” or “in the direction of.” We were going <em>to</em> buy some popcorn; my cat is going <em>to</em> the window. <em>Too</em> means “in excess” or “also.” I ate <em>too</em> many cookies this year and he did, <em>too</em>.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Anything involving <em>two</em> uses <em>tw</em> somewhere in the word: between, twice, twins, twenty, intertwine, and more.</p>

</details>

### 8. Affect/Effect — Affect / Effect

💡 **Giải thích:** Khoảng 80% trường hợp: ***affect*** là **động từ** (ảnh hưởng tới), ***effect*** là **danh từ** (kết quả, tác động). Ngoại lệ: *effect change* (động từ) = **tạo ra** thay đổi; *affect* (danh từ, tâm lý học) = biểu hiện cảm xúc.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Ooh, I love those sound *affects*. | Ooh, I love those sound effects. |
| Their son didn’t consider whether eating that much candy might *effect* him. | Their son didn't consider whether eating that much candy might affect him. |
| It is time for us all to *affect* change in our own lives. | It is time for us all to effect change in our own lives. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Of all the word pairs separated by a single vowel, this pair is the worst for native speakers and language learners alike. Between the two words, there are four different meanings: <em>Affect</em> is both a verb (to influence) and a noun (emotional expression), and <em>effect</em> is both a verb (to make happen) and a noun (impact).</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>About 80 percent of the time, you can trust that <em>affect</em> is a verb. “Eating a lot of candy might <em>affect</em> you. It <em>affected</em> me greatly.” Similarly, <em>effect</em> is usually a noun. If you love a particular sound <em>effect</em> such as a cowbell, that <em>effect</em> is a single item—a noun (a cowbell, in this case). An <em>effect</em> is an impact: “Wow! That cowbell had quite the <em>effect</em> on the audience!”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>In spite of the tempting rule that <em>affect</em> = verb and <em>effect</em> = noun, there are exceptions. <em>Affect</em> is also a noun, and <em>effect</em> is also a verb. <em>Affect</em> (noun) is used in psychology, and refers to emotional display. People with “flat affect” appear emotionless, even if they aren’t. To <em>effect</em> (verb) is to bring about or make happen. You, too, can <em>effect</em> change in your life.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>In what order should you use <em>affect</em> and <em>effect</em> in the following sentence? The beautiful lighting ---------- in the clinic ---------- ed the patients greatly; they made me wish to ---------- changes in our policy because our own patients show little ---------- at all. Lighting <em>effects</em> can <em>affect</em> patients. To <em>effect</em> a change might change a person’s <em>affect</em>.</p>

</details>

### 9. Ending a Sentence with a Preposition — Kết thúc câu bằng giới từ

💡 **Giải thích:** Đây là "lỗi" **giả**: quy tắc cấm kết thúc câu bằng giới từ đã lỗi thời. Trong văn nói và hầu hết văn viết, *Who was he talking to?* hoàn toàn tự nhiên. Chỉ khi viết cho người cực kỳ khó tính mới cần dời giới từ lên trước (*to whom…*).

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The rules of grammar are a lot to keep track <em>of</em>, especially when you hear that prepositions are something you’re never supposed to end a sentence <em>with</em>. The cold, hard truth is that we do end sentences with prepositions in casual speech, in most written material, and in our minds.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Language evolves and speech affects the way people write, so some rules fade. If you are writing for a straight-edge grammarian, tuck those prepositions inside a sentence, as in “I need to hang <em>up</em> my coat,” “She has much <em>for</em> which to be grateful,” and “We wondered <em>to</em> whom you were talking.” Otherwise, let those prepositions fly.</p>
<p><b>❌ (Bị cho là) sai</b></p>
<p>• I need to hang my coat <em>up</em>; which closet can I put it <em>in</em>?</p>
<p>• She sure has a lot to be grateful <em>for</em>.</p>
<p>• Who was he talking <em>to</em>?</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Winston Churchill was famously alleged to have joked, “Ending a sentence with a preposition is something up with which I will not put.” It has appeared in many variants, and is attributed to dozens of people (and character types), but it’s a joke about a pedantic rule that has little bearing on real life. You may let it go, dear reader, and be in good company.</p>

</details>

### 10. Starting a Sentence with a Conjunction — Bắt đầu câu bằng liên từ (And, But, Or)

💡 **Giải thích:** Bắt đầu câu bằng *And/But/Or* **không sai**, nhưng câu phải **liên quan logic** với câu trước. Ở các ví dụ, câu sau không nối tiếp ý câu trước nên đọc rất lạc lõng. Hãy bỏ liên từ hoặc viết lại cho mạch lạc.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I’m staying here. *And* what was life like when you were a kid? | I'm staying here. What was life like when you were a kid? |
| *But* let me tell you: I have a long story about that. | Let me tell you: I have a long story about that. |
| *Or* we could go out for drinks. | We could go out for drinks. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Conjunction People are often shocked to learn that most of the time it’s fine to start a sentence with a conjunction. <em>And</em> it works much better if the sentence is closely related to the previous one. The main question is whether the sentence is clear and complete.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The sentences in the first example appear unrelated; starting the second sentence with <em>and</em> makes it seem as if it’s supposed to follow naturally. <em>But</em> it doesn’t. In the second example, the speaker starts a story with <em>but</em>. It’s awkward, unless the speaker is using <em>but</em> to interrupt a thought and launch a different one. In the third example, <em>or</em> has no prior sentence, so the reader doesn’t know the alternative.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Starting sentences with conjunctions, particularly when they refer to a previous sentence, is not a problem. You’re on solid ground with either “I thought I liked him, but he was a jerk,” or “I thought I liked him. <em>But</em> he was a jerk.” The choice is really one of tone or style. <em>And</em> you can correct those who try to correct you. Don’t start a paragraph with a conjunction, though.</p>

</details>

### 11. Misplaced Modifiers — Bổ ngữ đặt sai chỗ (misplaced modifiers)

💡 **Giải thích:** Cụm bổ nghĩa phải đặt **sát** từ mà nó bổ nghĩa, nếu không câu sẽ mang nghĩa buồn cười: *ice cream sweating from the marathon* (kem đổ mồ hôi), *ancient daughter* (cô con gái cổ đại).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| She handed him the ice cream, *sweating from the marathon*. | Sweating from the marathon, she handed him the ice cream. |
| He searched all over for his *ancient daughter’s toy*. | He searched all over for his daughter's ancient toy. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Misplacing your modifier can result in unexpected and startling mistakes, such as the humorous line from Groucho Marx: “I shot an elephant in my pajamas.” These can be hard to spot because you know what you were trying to say, even if nobody else does!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A modifier calls attention to something or someone. In the corrected sentence “Sweating from the marathon, she handed him the ice cream,” the correct placement of the phrase “sweating from the marathon” lets you know that it describes the woman (rather than the ice cream). A <em>misplaced</em> modifier directs attention to the wrong place in a sentence, as in “He drove his dog to day care, <em>prepared for the faculty meeting</em>.” No dog is prepared for what goes on at faculty meetings. It should be: “<em>Prepared for the faculty meeting</em>, he drove his dog to day care.”</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Decades ago, many people learned to diagram sentences, a technique that visually shows the grammatical structure of a sentence. It was a clear way to learn that a modifier has to be right next to the thing it modifies. If she drove her dog to doggie day care wearing nothing but heels, the diagram would show that the modifier is next to the dog, not next to her.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which noun does the modifier “<em>they call Prince</em>” modify: the horse, or the sister? He bought a horse for his sister they call Prince. While his sister <em>might</em> be called Prince, it’s more likely to be the horse’s name. He bought his sister a horse they call Prince.</p>

</details>

### 12. Dangling Modifiers — Bổ ngữ lơ lửng (dangling modifiers)

💡 **Giải thích:** Cụm mở đầu kiểu *Driving down the road,…* phải bổ nghĩa cho **chủ ngữ ngay sau dấu phẩy**. *Driving down the road, a deer…* nghĩa là con nai đang lái xe! Chủ ngữ thực sự phải xuất hiện rõ ràng trong câu.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Driving down the road*, a deer watched him carefully. | Driving down the road, he noticed a deer watching him carefully. |
| *Youthful misdemeanors* can come back to haunt you while job hunting *.* | While you are job hunting, youthful misdemeanors can come back to haunt you. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Unlike a misplaced modifier, which directs attention to the wrong part of the sentence, a dangling modifier has nothing to modify. They are complete sentences, but they make no sense.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Modifiers offer an opportunity to go into greater detail about a subject, but the subject must be explicitly present in the sentence. Sometimes the solution is to add the missing subject, as in the first example: “As <em>he</em> drove down the road, a deer watched him carefully.” Now it’s clear that the deer wasn’t driving down the road. In the second example, youthful misdemeanors aren’t job hunting; <em>you</em> are.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>A modifier, as an actor within a sentence, makes a small change to that sentence. It is usually descriptive, as in “dressed like a penguin,” and is intended to deepen or clarify a sentence so that the reader recognizes subtlety of detail. “Dressed like a penguin, John reluctantly removed his cowboy hat and took his wife’s hand as they joined the party.” We can surmise that he was probably wearing a tuxedo; the modifier shines a light on his feelings.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>What does the following sentence need for clarity? Coming up the stairs, the fireworks went off in all directions. We need to know <em>who</em> is coming up the stairs; we know it isn't the fireworks.</p>

</details>

### 13. Possessive Pronouns with Gerunds — Đại từ sở hữu + V-ing (danh động từ)

💡 **Giải thích:** Trước một **danh động từ** (V-ing dùng như danh từ), dùng **tính từ sở hữu**: *my going, his reading* (việc tôi đi, việc anh ấy đọc), không dùng *me going, him reading* trong văn viết chuẩn.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Because of *me going* to the store today, I will have food for dinner. | Because of my going to the store today, I will have food for dinner. |
| They always teased John about *him reading*. | They always teased John about his reading. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This one can be tricky to get right, because the incorrect version can <em>sound</em> right to our ears. “I appreciate <em>you</em> filling in for me, Rebekah.” The problem is that it’s wrong. The trick is to identify the action.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Identify the action: I went to the store, so the going-to-the-store action was <em>mine</em>, all mine. It was <em>my going</em> to the store that enabled me to make dinner. The easy fix is “Because <em>I</em> went to the store today, <em>I</em> will have food for dinner.” John was teased about <em>his</em> action; it was <em>his reading</em> that caused them to tease him, so he was teased “about <em>his</em> reading.”</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>A gerund is an –ing verb used as a noun (bellyaching, pontificating, roaring, etc.). They are often paired with possessive pronouns: <em>your</em> filling in, <em>her</em> roaring<em>, his</em> bellyaching. Once you understand who the action belongs to, you will remember; it will be <em>your</em> (possessive pronoun) <em>remembering</em> (gerund) that guides you to safe and correct usage.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which pronouns should you use in the following sentence: <em>we</em>, <em>us</em>, or <em>our</em>? ---------- dancing with wild abandon is what got ---------- into trouble in the first place. It was <em>our</em> dancing with abandon that got <em>us</em> into trouble.</p>

</details>

### 14. Dual Possession — Sở hữu kép (của hai người)

💡 **Giải thích:** Hai người **cùng sở hữu một thứ** → chỉ thêm *'s* vào **người sau**: *Avery and Morgan's cat*. Mỗi người sở hữu riêng → thêm *'s* cho cả hai: *Avery's and Morgan's cats*. Khi có đại từ, dùng dạng sở hữu: *Peter's and her car*, *Karen's and my correspondence*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Our dog pounced on *Avery’s and Morgan’s* cat with significant consequences. | Our dog pounced on Avery and Morgan's cat with significant consequences. |
| They came to look at *Peter’s and she’s* car but bought another one instead. | They came to look at Peter's and her car but bought another one instead. |
| *Karen’s and mine’s* stealthy correspondence lasted many years. | Karen's and my stealthy correspondence lasted many years. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Two kinds of dual possession—with two names, and with a name and a pronoun—are stumbling blocks for even the smartest writers. Is it <em>Elaine’s and I’s</em> coffee pot? <em>Jill’s and Jack’s</em> pail of water? The rules are specific; you just need to follow them.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>When two people own something together—call them Avery and Morgan—then what they own is Avery and <em>Morgan’s</em> property, with the apostrophe at the end of the second name of the unit of Avery and Morgan. If it’s Avery’s and <em>his</em> property (using a pronoun), the apostrophe is placed at the end of the first name of the unit of Avery and him; the possessive is already there in the pronoun <em>his</em>.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Does the sentence have two names? The apostrophe goes on the second name: Jan and <em>Robert’s</em> pizza. Does the sentence have a name plus a pronoun? An apostrophe belongs on the name only: <em>Jan’s</em> and my pizza. Never use an apostrophe with a possessive pronoun (“my’s”).</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Where would you place the apostrophes in the following sentence? We ate Eric_ and Michelle_ pizza before we even made it to Lori_ and his house. The pair, “Eric and Michelle,” is a unit: Eric and <em>Michelle’s</em> pizza. “<em>Lori’s</em> and his” house is a unit as well.</p>

</details>

### 15. Double Genitive — Sở hữu cách kép (a friend of mine)

💡 **Giải thích:** Cấu trúc **of + sở hữu**: *a friend of **mine***, *an acquaintance of **my uncle's***. Ý là "một trong số những người quen của chú tôi". Phải có *'s* sau danh từ, giống như phải nói *of mine* chứ không phải *of me*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I remember that guy; he is an acquaintance *of my uncle*. | I remember that guy; he is an acquaintance of my uncle's. |
| Hey, that mug is one *of my roommate*. | Hey, that mug is one of my roommate's. |
| He is a colleague *of Ruth*. | He is a colleague of Ruth's. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You know what a double genitive is, even if you don’t realize it. “He’s <em>a friend of Maud’s</em>” is an example of a double genitive. Are you a “friend of Paul,” or a “friend of Paul’s”? Are you a “cousin of Jamal,” or a “cousin of Jamal’s”?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>He is an acquaintance of <em>my uncle’s</em>, not of mine. My roommate has many mugs, and that mug is his—<em>my roommate’s</em>. There is some subtlety here: The apostrophe places the focus on the person. “A colleague of Ruth’s” indicates that <em>Ruth</em> is the one who considers him the colleague. He is a colleague of <em>hers</em>; he is a colleague of <em>Ruth’s</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>This grammatical construction is oddly controversial, but it needn’t be. A double genitive indicates possession by the preposition <em>of</em> followed by the possessive form of a noun, pronoun, or name. As soon as you use <em>of</em>, as in <em>many of</em>, the <em>of</em> indicates possession: <em>of mine</em>, <em>one of his</em>, <em>many of the school’s</em>. Adding the apostrophe plus the letter <em>s</em> indicates that second level of possession. It is an idiomatic construction, and it is correct. Substitute a possessive pronoun (mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, yours, and theirs) for the name and the apostrophe plus the letter <em>s,</em> and you’ll still be right.</p>

</details>

### 16. Subjunctive Mood I: If I Were … — Thức giả định I: If I were…

💡 **Giải thích:** Với điều kiện **không có thật / giả định**, dùng ***were*** cho **mọi ngôi**: *If I were, if he were, if she were*. *If he was* chỉ đúng khi nói về một khả năng thật trong quá khứ.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *If he was* going to steal that TV from you, he did a lousy job of it. | If he were going to steal that TV from you, he did a lousy job of it. |
| *If our professor was* to assign grammar homework, we would all protest. | If our professor were to assign grammar homework, we would all protest. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Did you know that verbs have moods? The subjunctive mood allows you to be wishful, or to talk about something that isn’t true. In <em>Fiddler on the Roof</em>, Tevye was not a rich man, but he sang about how he would spend his money <em>if he were</em> actually rich.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A sentence of this type can be wide open in time, and it can address unreal possibilities. Regardless of who is speculating, use <em>were</em>: “If he <em>were</em> going to steal that TV,” or “If your professor <em>were</em> to assign homework.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>When you use this construction—<em>if ---------- were</em> —you open up the likelihood that something (good or bad) would have happened, might be happening, or will be happening. <em>If</em> he <em>were</em> here yesterday, he would have seen that error coming. <em>If</em> you <em>were</em> here right now, you could stop that error. <em>If</em> you <em>were</em> here tomorrow, you might stop that error. <em>If</em> you <em>weren’t</em> here, too bad.</p>

</details>

### 17. Subjunctive Mood II: I Demand That — Thức giả định II: I demand that…

💡 **Giải thích:** Sau các động từ **yêu cầu, đề nghị, khăng khăng** (*insist, recommend, demand, suggest, command…*) + *that*, động từ ở **dạng nguyên mẫu không chia** cho mọi ngôi: *that he **leave***, *that the committee **approve***, *that they **be***.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| She insists *that he leaves* now because he corrected her grammar. | She insists that he leave now because he corrected her grammar. |
| Management recommends *that the committee approves* the project | Management recommends that the committee approve the project. |
| He used to command *that his underlings are* at attention at all times. | He used to command that his underlings be at attention at all times. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>… The subjunctive mood expresses a particular condition: “I hope <em>that</em> you...,” “I suggest <em>that</em> she...,” and “it’s important <em>that</em> I...” In each case, <em>that</em> sets up an expectation for the remainder of the sentence. The challenge comes in the verb that follows that opening. Is it essential that he <em>write</em> well, or <em>writes</em> well?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The tendency in this error is to conjugate the verb: “that he <em>leaves</em>,” “that the committee <em>approves</em>,” etc. However, the verb after <em>that</em> should always be unconjugated: leave, approve, be. She insists that he <em>leave</em> now. Management recommends that the committee <em>approve</em> the project. He commanded that his underlings <em>be</em> at attention. It’s simple; I hope that you <em>learn</em>.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>A lovely consistency of this aspect of the subjunctive mood is that when it expresses commands, suggestions, and wishes, the verb that follows (eat, stir, watch, etc.) is always in present tense, regardless of the tense of the rest of the sentence. She <em>insisted</em> that you leave (past tense), she <em>insists</em> that you leave (present tense), and she <em>will insist</em> that you leave (future tense) all use “leave.”</p>

</details>

### 18. May and Might — May và Might

💡 **Giải thích:** *May* dùng cho điều **có thể thật sự xảy ra** (khả năng cao hơn) hoặc **xin phép**. *Might* dùng cho điều **giả định**, ít chắc chắn hơn, hoặc **đã không xảy ra**: *She might have run for president* (đã có thể ra tranh cử, nhưng không).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We *might* sit down to eat dinner. | We may sit down to eat dinner. |
| She *may* have run for president. | She might have run for president (but she didn't). |
| The coffee *might* cool down during your meeting. | The coffee may cool down during your meeting. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Oh, the possibilities! If you use these two words interchangeably when you speak, you are not alone. <em>May</em> and <em>might</em> deal with things that are, were, or will be possible; there are subtle differences between the two that are worth knowing.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>May</em> is used for things that really could happen, as in “I <em>may</em> be correct.” <em>Might</em> is for things that are hypothetical, as in “I <em>might</em> have driven a little too enthusiastically.” It’s likely that you will sit down to eat dinner, so use <em>may</em>. Few of us know people who have run for president, so use <em>might</em>. And that coffee will definitely cool down, so use <em>may.</em> If it’s probable, use <em>may</em>. If it’s unlikely or only vaguely possible, use <em>might</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Consider the famous children’s poem: “Star light, star bright, first star I see tonight; I wish I may, I wish I might, have the wish I wish tonight.” It trained us to use <em>may</em> and <em>might</em> the same way. But if you take it (and your wish) seriously and practice daily, you <em>may</em> get your wish to perform at Carnegie Hall. If you never practice except in the shower, you <em>might</em>.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>may</em> or <em>might</em> in the following sentence? I just ---------- have climbed Mt. Kilimanjaro in my pajamas; you weren’t there so you don’t know. Mt. Kilimanjaro is cold on top, so you would have to wear more than pajamas! You just <em>might</em> have climbed Mt. Kilimanjaro.</p>

</details>

### 19. Get, Got, and Gotten — Get, Got và Gotten

💡 **Giải thích:** *Get – got – got/gotten*. ***Have got*** = có (*I've got*), ***have got to*** = phải (*I've got to go*, viết tắt văn nói *gotta*). Trong tiếng Anh Mỹ, *gotten* là quá khứ phân từ chỉ **quá trình có được**. Không bỏ *have* trong văn viết, và sau *did* dùng **get** (nguyên mẫu).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *I got* a twenty that says you mess up its and it’s. | I've got a twenty that says you mess up its and it's. |
| He’s *gotta* plan. | He's got to plan. / He has got a plan. |
| When did you *got* here? | When did you get here? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This word and its variants are incredibly common and useful, and rather easily misused. It means to receive, to become, to be allowed or to have the opportunity, and to arrive. It also means possession, understanding, and obligation when paired with “have”! That is one busy word! (Have you) got it?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Get</em> —present tense—seems fairly straightforward: I <em>get</em> paid, I <em>get</em> restless, and I <em>get</em> there on time. Adding “have” to its past participle —<em>got</em> —gives us the urgency of “I must”: “I <em>have got</em> to buy books,” or “I <em>have got</em> to start working.” Adding “have” to <em>gotten</em>, however, serves as the standard past participle in American English: “I <em>have gotten</em> [received] a gift,” “I <em>have gotten</em> [become] sick,” “I <em>have gotten</em> [had the opportunity] to visit Yosemite,” and “I <em>have gotten</em> [arrived] to work on time.” The complication is when to use which one. If you speak American English, use <em>have gotten</em> as the past participle.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Gotten</em> is a legitimate word; used with “have” (often in a contraction, as in “<em>I’ve gotten</em> this project off the ground”), it makes good sense to Americans and Canadians. Although it is English in origin, speakers of British English abhor its use; it fell out of favor several hundred years ago. To the English ear, it sounds like an encroaching Americanism, but it is not.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>got</em> or <em>gotten</em> in the following sentence? I have ---------- to visit Angkor Wat and Borobudur before I die! This sentence uses the <em>have got</em> construction to indicate obligation. I <em>must</em>! If the sentence read “I have gotten to visit [A and B] several times,” it would also be correct. I <em>was allowed</em>.</p>

</details>

### 20. Indefinite Pronouns: Each — Đại từ bất định: Each

💡 **Giải thích:** *Each* (kể cả *each of the…*, *each one of…*) luôn là **số ít** → động từ chia số ít: ***Each** of the students **was**…*, dù danh từ phía sau là số nhiều.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Each of the three students *were* entitled to one grammatical error on the quiz. | Each of the three students was entitled to one grammatical error on the quiz. |
| While I’m watching, each one of the kittens *are* falling asleep. | While I'm watching, each one of the kittens is falling asleep. |
| Each of their grammatical errors *feel* like a knife through my heart. | Each of their grammatical errors feels like a knife through my heart. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Each reader of this book <em>is</em> entitled to learn about grammar, right? Or wait, should that be each of us <em>are</em> entitled to learn about grammar? Is this about me, or all of us?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>This is actually a simple rule to learn! No matter how many numbers come after <em>each</em>, the verb must be singular: Each one of the students was entitled to an error. Ignore the number and simply connect <em>each</em> to the singular form of the verb. <em>Each</em> student <em>is</em> entitled, <em>each</em> kitten <em>is</em> falling asleep, and <em>each</em> error <em>feels</em> like a knife. Ouch! The word <em>each</em> indicates just one of two or more; that’s why it is singular.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The word <em>each</em> is an indefinite pronoun, along with <em>all</em>, <em>any</em>, <em>some</em>, <em>none</em>, and many others. Some are singular (<em>each</em>), some are plural (<em>several</em>), and some can be both, as in <em>there is none left</em> and <em>there are none left</em>. Perhaps the confusion in singular/plural words such as <em>none</em>, <em>all</em>, and <em>most</em> has resulted in <em>each</em> appearing equally confusing, but it isn’t. <em>Each</em> uses a singular construction.</p>

</details>

### 21. Helping Verbs: To Have — Trợ động từ: To Have (thì hoàn thành)

💡 **Giải thích:** Thì hoàn thành = *have/has/had* + **quá khứ phân từ** (V3): *had **eaten*** (không phải *had ate*). *Seen* là V3 nên phải có *have*: *I **saw** / I **have seen***. Dùng **quá khứ hoàn thành** cho hành động xảy ra **trước** một hành động quá khứ khác.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Apparently he *had ate* my dinner when he broke in. | Apparently he had eaten my dinner when he broke in. |
| *I seen* him when he crept out from the kitchen. | I saw him when he crept out from the kitchen. |
| Zahra *agreed* to wait at the shop before she realized that her purse was at home. | Zahra had agreed to wait at the shop before she realized that her purse was at home. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The simple past—<em>fell</em>, <em>broke</em>, <em>forgot</em>, <em>regretted</em>, etc.—is simple: The lamp <em>fell</em> and <em>broke</em>. I <em>forgot</em> the milk. Errors can creep in, however, when the helping verb <em>to have</em> is needed.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The perfect form indicates that an action has been completed. “He <em>had eaten</em> my dinner” means that dinner is now gone. Because this construction adds an extra step in understanding, some speakers leave it out altogether, resulting in “I seen him.” Others add the correct version of <em>have</em>, but neglect to shift the verb, resulting in “He had ate.” When two parts of a sentence occur at different times, it can be tempting to put both parts of the sentence in the past tense, but “Zahra <em>had agreed</em>” before she realized that she didn’t have her purse.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Adding a variant of <em>have</em> to a word in past tense turns it into a “perfect” construction. I <em>have fallen</em> (present perfect), I <em>had fallen</em> (past perfect), and I <em>will have fallen</em> (future perfect) are all variants. Some verbs change when the sentence shifts into perfect tense: <em>fall</em>, <em>have fallen</em>; <em>ate</em>, <em>have eaten</em>; <em>saw</em>, <em>have seen</em>, etc., but most stay the same (<em>regretted</em>, <em>have regretted</em>).</p>

</details>

### 22. Helping Verbs: To Be — Trợ động từ: To Be (thì tiếp diễn)

💡 **Giải thích:** Thì tiếp diễn = **chủ ngữ + am/is/are/was/were + V-ing**. Không bỏ *be* (*They winning*), không dùng *be* nguyên mẫu (*I be sitting*), và động từ chính phải có **-ing** (*are drink*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *They winning* at cards all evening. | They were winning at cards all evening. |
| I *be sitting* next to the fire, just like a pirate. ARRR! | I am sitting next to the fire, just like a pirate. ARRR! |
| You *are drink* cider again; I hope it’s good. | You are drinking cider again; I hope it's good. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p><em>Be</em> stands alone as a verb, and its presence changes other verbs from (for example) “I <em>read</em>” to “I <em>am reading</em>.” You <em>are reading</em> this entry, since you’ve <em>been wanting</em> to know more about grammar, and you still need help! We all do. Even verbs can need help; that’s why <em>to be</em> —the most common verb in English—is one of the helping verbs.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Native speakers of English usually combine a subject (a noun such as a person, place, or thing), a verb (some variant of <em>to be</em>), and a present participle (going, doing, dancing, etc.) to create sentences such as “They <em>are drinking</em> cider.” In some parts of the United States, people leave out the “to be” part, so rather than “They <em>are winning</em>,” they would say “They <em>winning</em>.” The correct form needs that helping verb!</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Irregular verbs are old. <em>To be</em> has to be one of the oldest, as it deals with our very existence; it definitely predates Shakespeare’s <em>Hamlet</em> (“to be or not to be?”) from about 1600. When we use <em>to be</em> with an – ing or present participle form of another verb, as in “I <em>am dancing</em> like a disco queen,” we are indicating continuous action; it’s called the progressive tense.</p>

</details>

### 23. Helping Verbs: To Do — Trợ động từ: To Do

💡 **Giải thích:** *Do* dùng với *I, you, we, they*; ***does*** dùng với **he, she, it** (ngôi 3 số ít). Quá khứ luôn là ***did / didn't*** cho mọi ngôi.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Does* you want some of this carrot cake? | Do you want some of this carrot cake? |
| He *do* think he *don’t* need any help with grammar. | He does think he doesn't need any help with grammar. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We use <em>do</em>, <em>does</em>, <em>did</em>, <em>don’t</em>, and <em>didn’t</em> constantly; because of that, we run the risk of messing up this very irregular auxiliary verb. Have you ever heard a child say that she “dood” something, as if it were a past tense version of <em>do</em>? You wouldn’t be alone in smiling at her mistake!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The past tense of <em>do</em> is always <em>did</em>, and the past tense of <em>don’t</em> is always <em>didn’t</em>. Simple! With <em>do</em> and <em>does</em>, we confuse them because of the irregularity of “I <em>do</em>” and “he does.” Using “he/she <em>don’t</em>” is the most common mistake of all; it should always be “he/she <em>doesn’t</em>.” I think he <em>doesn’t</em> need any help with grammar.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The word <em>do</em> has so many uses! We use it to ask questions (“<em>Do</em> you like music?”), to substitute for repetitive phrases (“She plays music and so <em>do</em> I”), to emphasize something (“I <em>did</em> do the dishes!”), and to express the negative and positive (“We <em>don’t</em> listen to music” and “Yes, you <em>do</em>”). Its frequent use means you’ll have lots of opportunities to get this right.</p>

</details>

### 24. Reflexive Pronouns: Myself — Đại từ phản thân: Myself

💡 **Giải thích:** *Myself, yourself, himself…* chỉ dùng khi **chủ ngữ và tân ngữ là một người** (*I hurt myself*) hoặc để **nhấn mạnh** (*I did it myself*). Không dùng *myself* thay cho *me* cho "sang". *Theyself* không tồn tại; dạng đúng là *themselves*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| The IRS sent the refund check to my wife and *myself*. | The IRS sent the refund check to my wife and me. |
| Be sure to notify Morgan and *myself* that you finished the paint job. | Be sure to notify Morgan and me that you finished the paint job. |
| Did you tell them that my paintings were better than those done by *theyself*? | Did you tell them that my paintings were better than those done by them (themselves)? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>It’s common to hear people use <em>myself</em> as a substitute for <em>me</em>. You might also have heard “win <em>theyself</em> a prize,” or “fix <em>you-self</em> some dinner.” It seems like a reasonable mistake when one uses a subject pronoun (they or you), but these need <em>reflexive</em> pronouns such as <em>themselves</em> or <em>yourself</em>.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A reflexive pronoun is useful in referring to the one who did the action: <em>myself</em>, <em>yourself</em> /<em>-selves</em>, <em>himself</em>, <em>herself</em>, <em>oneself</em>, <em>itself</em>, <em>ourselves</em>, and <em>themself</em> /<em>-selves</em>. For example, “I hurt <em>myself</em>” includes the subject, the verb, and the (reflexive) object. It must acquire meaning from an earlier part of the sentence. “<em>You</em> cooked it <em>yourself</em>.” Sentences such as “Please give it to <em>myself</em>” are incorrect because <em>myself</em> doesn’t refer to anyone. The IRS sent that check to me. I didn’t do it! Neither Morgan nor I finished the paint job—you did!</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>If you use the phrase, “I’ve got to hand it to <em>myself</em>,” that is exactly right; you did the action right back to yourself. “I’m going to decorate the room <em>myself</em>, and laugh <em>myself</em> into delirium” are completely appropriate. After all, Annie Lennox and Aretha Franklin sang in 1985 that “Sisters are doin’ it for <em>themselves</em>”; <em>they</em> (pronoun) and <em>themselves</em> (reflexive pronoun).</p>

</details>

### 25. Double Negatives — Phủ định kép

💡 **Giải thích:** Trong tiếng Anh chuẩn, **hai từ phủ định** trong một mệnh đề triệt tiêu nhau hoặc gây khó hiểu. *Don't give me no nonsense* là văn nói địa phương; văn chuẩn dùng *any*. Câu *She didn't want me to not do it* rối rắm, nên viết lại cho rõ nghĩa khẳng định.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| “She *didn’t* want me to *not* do it.” | She wanted me to do it. |
| *Don’t* give me *no* nonsense. | Don't give me any nonsense. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We can blame the Rolling Stones for teaching us that we “<em>can’t</em> get <em>no</em> satisfaction,” and Bob Dylan for writing the very catchy “You Ain’t Going Nowhere.” Those double negatives, and their variants, are ubiquitous in popular usage. Their use sometimes forces you to stop and puzzle out the positive meaning, which is never fun.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A double negative can be a simple intensifier, as in “We <em>didn’t</em> do <em>nothing</em> on Saturday,” or as a substitute for <em>any</em> or <em>anything</em>. We emphasize the word <em>nothing</em> to make it stronger. If you were to tell someone, “I <em>don’t not</em> want you to ask me on a date,” the sentence is just plain passive-aggressive (and unlikely to win you a date). Look at how difficult it is to know what that first example is communicating! The proper form is much easier to understand: “She wanted me to do it.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>There are situations and needs that make a double negative useful. Because we often imagine that two wrongs do make a right, using two negatives can sometimes result in a positive, and the spoken emphasis makes that clear: I didn’t <em>not</em> want that muffin. Sometimes the double negative serves a purpose in dramatically emphasizing a point. In <em>The Wizard of Oz</em>, Dorothy and friends arrive at the Emerald City, where they are memorably greeted with a shouted “<em>Not nobody</em>! <em>Not no how</em>!” and a slamming window.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Does this double negative work well, or is it an error? That attitude <em>won’t</em> get you <em>nowhere</em>. What? Should this be “That attitude <em>won’t</em> get you <em>anywhere</em>?” Or is it an awkward and confusing way of saying that it <em>will</em> get you <em>somewhere?</em> Assume the former.</p>

</details>

## Chương 2: Dấu câu (Punctuation Saves Lives)

### 26. Comma Usage — Dấu phẩy

💡 **Giải thích:** Đừng đặt dấu phẩy ở mọi chỗ ngắt hơi. Quy tắc chính: **không** tách chủ ngữ và động từ; dùng dấu phẩy quanh **thông tin bổ sung không thiết yếu** (*My friends, Roderick and Anna, …* nếu bạn chỉ có hai người bạn đó); dấu phẩy đặt **trước** liên từ nối hai mệnh đề, không đặt sau.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| The book, was lying right there; it needed to be finished. | The book was lying right there; it needed to be finished. |
| My friends Roderick and Anna were getting married. | My friends, Roderick and Anna, were getting married. |
| At first we wanted to eat but, we went home instead. | At first we wanted to eat, but we went home instead. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Sprinkling commas through one’s writing as if they were candy is not a good practice. Similarly, withholding said “candy” leaves one’s readers galloping too fast through the field of prose without a chance to breathe. Commas add rhythm and beauty, not to mention clarity, to one’s written ideas.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Many writers place commas wherever you might pause naturally when speaking. That’s often fine, but there are specific rules to follow, and they’re worth learning. Use a comma to separate independent clauses when they’re joined by a conjunction (and, but, for, or, nor, so, yet). “At first we wanted to eat, but we went home instead.” Use a pair of commas mid-sentence to set off clauses, phrases, and words that aren’t essential. “My friends, Roderick and Anna, were getting married.” Use commas to separate items in a series. “Buy apples, oranges, and bananas.” Use a comma after an introductory clause, phrase, or word that comes before the main clause. “If I have time, I would really like to learn to play the banjo.” Commas should never appear between a verb and its subject, as in “The book, was lying right there.”</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Mention the Oxford comma at your peril! People have strong feelings about it, and this is often a result of their training. Journalists are taught never to use it. The Oxford comma, otherwise known as the serial comma, is the final comma in a series that comes before the word <em>and</em> or <em>or</em>. “Buy apples, oranges, and bananas” uses the Oxford comma. You’ll never be wrong if you use it, and you’ll avoid troubling sentences such as “I invited my parents, Joseph Stalin and Mayor Susan Smith.”</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Where would you place commas in this sentence? My_friend_Billy_who_hates_grammar_ quizzes_failed_the_grammar_quiz. My friend Billy, who hates grammar quizzes, failed the grammar quiz.</p>

</details>

### 27. Comma Splice — Lỗi nối câu bằng dấu phẩy (comma splice)

💡 **Giải thích:** Hai **mệnh đề độc lập** không được nối chỉ bằng dấu phẩy. Cách sửa: dùng **dấu chấm**, **dấu chấm phẩy**, hoặc **dấu phẩy + liên từ** (*and, but, so…*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Hand me the salt and pepper, the eggs are bland. | Hand me the salt and pepper; the eggs are bland. |
| You sat on my pastry, now I can’t eat it. | You sat on my pastry, so now I can't eat it. |
| The birds come to the feeder every day, I have to refill it constantly. | The birds come to the feeder every day. I have to refill it constantly. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>A comma splice occurs when you divide two complete clauses with a comma. Although we may use this phrasing when we speak, it is always a mistake when you write.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>When two clauses are related, they should be joined by a comma and a conjunction, or divided by either a semicolon or a period (if they belong in two separate sentences). If you examine the incorrect examples, you will notice that—in addition to the solution of using either a semicolon or a period—you might alternatively try using a conjunction such as <em>because</em>, <em>so</em>, or <em>and</em>. “You sat on my pastry, <em>so</em> now I can’t eat it” works. “You sat on my pastry; now I can’t eat it” also works.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Although a semicolon is a handy replacement in a comma splice for related clauses, one should never separate unrelated clauses with a semicolon. “The birds come to the feeder every day; tomorrow I leave for Zimbabwe” may have a nice ring to it, but the clauses have nothing to do with each other.</p>

</details>

### 28. Apostrophes for Pluralization — Dùng dấu nháy (') cho số nhiều

💡 **Giải thích:** Số nhiều chỉ thêm **-s / -es**, **không** thêm dấu nháy. Dấu nháy dùng cho **sở hữu** (*the cat's toy*) và **rút gọn** (*let's = let us*). *Lets* (động từ chia ngôi 3) khác *let's*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Reading *let’s* us understand other people. | Reading lets us understand other people. |
| He kept seven *cat’s* in his home. | He kept seven cats in his home. |
| All the *neighbor’s* sat around in folding *chair’s*, watching the *firework’s*. | All the neighbors sat around in folding chairs, watching the fireworks. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We add <em>s</em> to nouns to make them plural: She runs with her <em>dogs</em> every morning. Adding an apostrophe before an <em>s</em> (her <em>dog’s</em>) to make a noun plural is not just a bad idea; it’s incorrect! Attention, sign makers everywhere!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>To write about more than one item, the primary rule is to add only <em>– s</em>. For words ending in <em>s</em>, <em>x</em>, <em>z</em>, <em>ch</em>, <em>ss</em>, or <em>sh</em>, you’ll need to add <em>–es</em>. Neither needs an apostrophe. This rule includes numbers (we lived there in the <em>’90s</em>), multiple uppercase letters (one can never have too many <em>CDs</em>), and names (three <em>Susans</em>). A word that is also a contraction, such as <em>let’s</em> (let us) requires just a moment’s thought.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The rule for apostrophe usage with pluralization is simple: no apostrophes! The only exception is made for clarity when you write about lowercase single letters: Mind your <em>p’s</em> and <em>q’s</em>; there are four <em>a’s</em> in Athabaskan; and you’ll need two <em>m’s</em> in dilemma. The <em>Oakland A’s</em> would also appreciate that apostrophe.</p>

</details>

### 29. Hyphens — Dấu gạch nối (hyphen)

💡 **Giải thích:** Dùng gạch nối để **nối các từ làm một tính từ đứng trước danh từ** (*a seven-year-old son*), với tiền tố *self-* (*self-aggrandizing*), phân số làm tính từ (*a quarter-cup*), và số ghép (*twenty-one*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| His boss is totally *self aggrandizing*. | His boss is totally self-aggrandizing. |
| She has a *seven year old* son. | She has a seven-year-old son. |
| I always use a *quarter cup* of semisweet chocolate chips in my cookies. | I always use a quarter-cup of semisweet chocolate chips in my cookies. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Hyphens have so many uses! They’re often used incorrectly, either appearing where they shouldn’t, or not being used when they should be. Along with comma usage, proper hyphenation is a common concern.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>With a hyphen you may join adjectives before a noun (<em>weak-armed</em> pitcher), with compound numbers (<em>seven-year-old</em>), with fractions <em>(a quarter-cup</em>), with a prefix connected to a proper noun (<em>un- American</em>), or to enhance clarity (<em>de-ice</em>). Lastly, use them with <em>half</em>, <em>all</em>, and <em>self</em> prefixes (<em>half-baked</em>, <em>all-in</em>, and <em>self-aggrandizing</em>). In each case, the hyphen connects two or more related words. The most important rule is that we use them to avoid confusion.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Don’t use a hyphen if you are using just one adjective; for example, she is an African American. With two adjectives, the hyphen is required: African-American woman. Adverbs ending in –ly do not take a hyphen (as in “quickly melting chocolate”). Language changes over time, and the hyphen has not been immune; for example, we used to eat <em>ice-cream</em> and tried to avoid being stung by <em>bumble-bees</em> in the summer. Those hyphens, and many others, have disappeared and the words were either separated (<em>ice cream</em>) or brought together (<em>bumblebee</em>). The internet age influences hyphen usage too; for example, words such as <em>hyperlink</em> and <em>toolbar</em> are unhyphenated because hyphens mess with coding issues.</p>

</details>

### 30. Capitalization — Viết hoa

💡 **Giải thích:** Viết hoa **chức danh đứng trước tên** (*Chancellor Jones*), không viết hoa khi đứng một mình (*the senator*). **Hướng** chỉ viết hoa khi là tên vùng (*the West*), không viết hoa khi chỉ hướng đi (*heading west*). **Mùa** không viết hoa. **Tôn giáo, ngôn ngữ, quốc tịch** luôn viết hoa.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Are you heading West in the Spring? | Are you heading west in the spring? |
| I saw chancellor Jones speaking with the Senator. | I saw Chancellor Jones speaking with the senator. |
| My friend is a buddhist and he speaks japanese. | My friend is a Buddhist and he speaks Japanese. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Noting which words should and should not be capitalized could be a full-time job. The all-or-nothing ones are simple: Always capitalize the days of the week, months, proper names, places, languages, and religions. Never capitalize the seasons. If only that were all you needed to know!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Professional titles and directions seem tricky, but the rules are actually quite simple. Capitalize titles before names, as in <em>Uncle</em> John or <em>President</em> Adrienne Smith. Do not capitalize a professional title when it appears alone (the <em>mayor</em>), or after a name, as in Ellen Jones, <em>company president</em>. For directions, your quick key is this: When you use <em>the</em> for a direction, capitalize that direction. Drive <em>northwest</em> to Seattle; you’ll be in <em>the West</em>.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Religions (Hinduism, Buddhism, etc.) and religious figures (Mohammed, Jesus, Buddha) are capitalized, but <em>atheist</em> and <em>pagan</em> are not.</p>

</details>

### 31. Quotation Marks — Dấu ngoặc kép

💡 **Giải thích:** Ngoặc kép đi **thành cặp** quanh đúng phần **lời nói trực tiếp**, trích dẫn, hoặc tên tác phẩm ngắn. Lời dẫn (*he said, she asked*) nằm ngoài ngoặc. Dấu chấm/phẩy (kiểu Mỹ) và dấu hỏi của câu trích nằm **trong** ngoặc.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My dog barked, “Hey, as if he were trying to tell us something.” | My dog barked, "Hey!" as if he were trying to tell us something. |
| Joe asked, Why not “buy some apples for dessert”? | Joe asked, "Why not buy some apples for dessert?" |
| To “bee or not to bee,” mused the queen of the hive. | "To bee or not to bee," mused the queen of the hive. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>In this age of “air quotes” around “words,” it can be hard to “know when” to use them properly. Don’t let this happen to you.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Quotation marks belong in pairs around very specific sets of words: direct speech (“Why not buy some apples for dessert?”), phrases, or (quite logically) quotations (“to bee or not to bee”). We often forget to close the quotation marks, and simply keep writing as if the quoted segment continues. An entire quoted phrase, including its original punctuation, needs to be within a pair of quotation marks. In American English, additional punctuation usually goes inside the quotation marks.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Americans and the British use punctuation differently with quotation marks. In American English, we write “policeman.” In British English, they write “bobby”. Note that the period lies inside the quotation marks for one and outside for the other. When punctuation is excluded from a quoted phrase, it remains outside: Did she finish her letter by writing, “I love you, Ned”? (The letter didn’t say “I love you, Ned?”.) For quotation marks within a quoted phrase, use single marks: The author told me, “My editor said, ‘We need an example here’ for this part of the book.”</p>

</details>

### 32. Em Dash and En Dash — Gạch dài (em dash —) và gạch ngắn (en dash –)

💡 **Giải thích:** **Em dash (—)**, dài nhất, thay cho ngoặc đơn, cặp dấu phẩy hoặc dấu hai chấm, để chèn hoặc nhấn mạnh ý. **En dash (–)** nối **khoảng số, khoảng thời gian** (*1769–1821*, *15–16 years old*). Gạch nối (-) chỉ nối từ ghép.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Napoleon (1769-1821) escaped from exile, for better or worse. | Napoleon (1769–1821) escaped from exile, for better or worse. |
| The Seahawks-Seattle’s football team-won the Super Bowl at last. | The Seahawks—Seattle's football team—won the Super Bowl at last. |
| They were 15—16 years old. | They were 15–16 years old. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Sometimes a dash isn’t just a dash. Do you know when to use a hyphen and when to use either an em or en dash? Did you know there were em and en dashes? It can be easy to confuse them, but they have unique uses.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Em dashes are the widest dash, and are so handy. They can replace parentheses—where an aside might appear—and that’s not all! They can also replace commas—as long as you have a pair—in many sentences. They can even replace a colon—like this. The slightly shorter en dash serves to join a pair of numbers (1769–1821 or 15–16) or to connect two ideas (such as the Canadian–American linguistic divide). Hyphens are often mistakenly used in place of en dashes, but they have their own uses, as you already learned.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>En dashes are the width of the letter n (–), and em dashes are the width of the letter m (—). To make them on your computer, learn these simple keystroke combinations: For Macs, press option + hyphen to make an en dash, and shift + option + hyphen (or use two hyphens) for em dash. On a PC, use ALT + 0150 (en dash) or ALT + 0151 (em dash).</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should the following sentence have an en dash or an em dash, or both? His little brother ---------- who should have known better ------- --- stole the cookie dough. You would need a pair of em dashes to set the internal phrase apart.</p>

</details>

### 33. Ellipses — Dấu ba chấm (ellipsis)

💡 **Giải thích:** Dấu ba chấm có **đúng 3 chấm** (…), dùng để chỉ **phần bị lược bỏ** trong trích dẫn hoặc **lời bỏ lửng**. Khi lược phần đầu câu trích, đặt … ở đầu. Không dùng thêm chấm thừa hay kết hợp với dấu hai chấm.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I told him that I wasn’t going to ever…...talk to him again. | I told him that I wasn't going to ever… talk to him again. |
| In the middle of her long speech, she said, “and that’s what I needed.” | In the middle of her long speech, she said, "…and that's what I needed." |
| He asked for just one thing:...... | He asked for just one thing… (hoặc: He asked for just one thing: silence.) |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>For all the fun of social media and the ease of texting, one unhappy consequence has been the overuse... of... lots of... periods. Put yourself on solid ground with this form of punctuation.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Whereas em dashes convey certainty—which we all would like to have when we wish to be taken seriously—ellipses can indicate some kind of... hesitancy. (Three dots please, not two or eight. Three.) Ellipses [plural] need a space on each side, but also (usually) between the three dots. They are also used to indicate that a quotation is just part of a longer passage: “... and that’s what I needed.”</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>The use of the terms “three dots” and (spoken) “dot dot dot” is so common that Herb Caen, late beloved columnist for the <em>San Francisco Chronicle</em>, called his style of using ellipses between short paragraphs “Three Dot Journalism.” The etymology of <em>ellipsis</em> is rooted in Greek; it means “omission.”</p>

</details>

### 34. Missing Accent Marks — Thiếu dấu trọng âm (từ mượn)

💡 **Giải thích:** Từ mượn từ tiếng Pháp, Tây Ban Nha… giữ **dấu đặc biệt** để chỉ cách phát âm: *résumé* (sơ yếu lý lịch, khác *resume* = tiếp tục), *jalapeño* (ñ đọc "nh"), *garçon* (ç đọc "s"). Dấu phải là dấu thật, không phải dấu nháy.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I gave the manager my re’sume’. | I gave the manager my résumé. |
| Wow! Those jalapenos really wake up the salsa! | Wow! Those jalapeños really wake up the salsa! |
| Garcon! Garcon! | Garçon! Garçon! |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Oh, the tragedy of writing about the delicious pate they served at last night’s party. Astute readers will imagine a head on a platter, all for want of the proper accent mark.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Accent marks, also known as diacritical marks, indicate the specific pronunciation of a letter. In garcon, for example, the <em>c</em> would be pronounced like <em>k</em>: Garkon! No wonder he doesn’t respond! Add a cedilla to that <em>c</em> and the sound changes: garçon. The server will respond to you now. You’d need a tilde over the <em>n</em> to spell and pronounce jalapeños correctly; and no matter how we might wish it to be true, apostrophes do not replace the two French accents. If you are typing in a country whose language uses these accent marks, you’ll find them on the keyboard.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Even if you’re not in a country where accent marks are common, you can still add them quite easily. If you’re on an Apple (Mac) laptop, or a cellphone (regardless of whether it is an iPhone or an Android), press and hold the key for which you need the accent. On a PC, unique four-digit codes, used after you press the ALT key, create various accents. Alternatively, you can use the “Insert” toolbar, and then select “Symbol.” Just don’t use an apostrophe.</p>

</details>

### 35. Semicolons — Dấu chấm phẩy (;)

💡 **Giải thích:** Dấu chấm phẩy nối **hai mệnh đề độc lập có liên quan**. **Không** dùng chấm phẩy để mở đầu danh sách (dùng dấu hai chấm), cũng không dùng sau một cụm không phải mệnh đề.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I like animals *;* lions, tigers, and bears. | I like animals: lions, tigers, and bears. |
| As for the Mariners baseball team *;* they lost again. | As for the Mariners baseball team, they lost again. |
| I like coffee *,* I would like to make some right now. | I like coffee; I would like to make some right now. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The much-maligned semicolon is your friend. Alas, some people use them as commas or colons, with incomplete clauses, or as part of winky-face emojis. Their true purpose, however, is to lend clarity and elegance only to those sentences in which they are needed.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A semicolon separates two related but independent clauses: “I like coffee<em>;</em> I would like to make some right now.” In a list preceded by a colon, a semicolon separates items that include commas: “I like hot drinks: coffee, because it helps me focus<em>;</em> and tea, because it reminds me of living in Ireland.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>When you have a coordinating conjunction between two clauses, you don’t need a semicolon. “He likes the red car, <em>but</em> she likes the gray car.” However, this sentence needs a semicolon: “He likes the red car<em>;</em> she likes the gray car.” And unlike with some colons, never capitalize the word following a semicolon.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use a semicolon in the following sentence? I should have stopped by the store for coffee ---------- I just didn’t bother. The first clause is complete; a semicolon is necessary.</p>

</details>

### 36. Possessives with Names Ending in S — Sở hữu cách của tên kết thúc bằng S

💡 **Giải thích:** Hãy **tin cách bạn nói**: ta nói *Yeats's poetry* (Yếts-iz), *James's mom* (Jêim-ziz) → viết **'s**. Tên *Yeats* có *s* là một phần của tên, đừng viết *Yeat's*. Chỉ danh từ **số nhiều** tận cùng -s mới chỉ thêm dấu nháy (*the Joneses' house*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I was startled by the attack on Yeat’s poetry. | I was startled by the attack on Yeats's poetry. |
| Jameses’ mom told him to sit down. | James's mom told him to sit down. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The Irish poet William Butler Yeats would roll over in his grave if he saw how often people wrote such egregious errors as “W.B. Yeat’s poetry.” Your mastery of possessives isn’t complete until you understand what to do when names end in <em>s.</em></p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>We are fortunate that spoken words came before written words, so we all have learned to say <em>Yeats’s poetry</em> and <em>James’s mom</em> before we learned to write them. Trust the way you speak! When a possessive name ends with <em>s</em> or <em>z</em>, add an apostrophe and an <em>s.</em> Say this to yourself: “I happen to love Yeats’s poetry.” You automatically said it correctly.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>When the final <em>s</em> in a word is unpronounced, as in Descartes, you simply add an apostrophe without the additional <em>s.</em> “Alas, they never understood <em>Descartes’</em> philosophies.” The same is true when a name ending in <em>s</em> is ancient (Socrates, Moses): Just add the apostrophe for the possessive. “I learned so much from <em>Socrates’</em> teachings.”</p>

</details>

### 37. Ending a Declarative Sentence with a Question Mark — Dùng dấu hỏi cho câu trần thuật

💡 **Giải thích:** Câu **gián tiếp** (*I asked myself if…*, *He wondered when…*) là câu **trần thuật**, kết thúc bằng **dấu chấm**. Chỉ câu hỏi trực tiếp mới dùng dấu hỏi.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I asked myself if this was true? | I asked myself if this was true. / I asked myself, "Is this true?" |
| He wondered when the train would arrive? | He wondered when the train would arrive. |
| Perhaps she questions her very existence? | Perhaps she questions her very existence. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Question Mark People often write declarative statements such as “I wonder who wrote <em>The Book of Love</em>?” The temptation to add a question mark is great, even though the sentence is not actually a question. If you wonder who wrote <em>The Book of Love</em>, just ask the question directly.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The solution for this mistake is to understand the difference between a question and a declarative sentence. Declarative sentences make a statement: “I wonder who wrote <em>The Book of Love</em>.” When you write a declarative sentence, regardless of the words in that sentence, you are not asking a question. Declare: “He wondered when the train would arrive.” Ask: “When will the train arrive?” Declare: “She asked her teacher if she might leave.” Ask: “She asked her teacher: May I leave?”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>There are four types of sentences: declarative (making a statement), interrogative (asking questions), imperative (issuing orders), and exclamative (self-explanatory!). You wonder. He asks. They question. I do declare!</p>

</details>

### 38. Colons — Dấu hai chấm (:)

💡 **Giải thích:** Dấu hai chấm giới thiệu **danh sách hoặc lời giải thích** đứng sau **một mệnh đề hoàn chỉnh**. Không đặt dấu hai chấm **giữa động từ và tân ngữ** (*I need: sugar*). Khi trích dẫn câu hoàn chỉnh sau dấu hai chấm, viết hoa chữ đầu.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| He gave me two things, a red wagon and a flag. | He gave me two things: a red wagon and a flag. |
| I need: sugar, flour, and baking powder. | I need sugar, flour, and baking powder. |
| In the words of Humphrey Bogart: “here’s looking at you, kid.” | In the words of Humphrey Bogart: "Here's looking at you, kid." |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Do you confuse the usage of colons and semicolons? They both separate parts of a sentence, but they fill quite different needs. They also come with their own punctuation rules: Pay attention!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A colon introduces whatever item or list of items comes next: a barrel of monkeys, a flotilla of geese, or a consternation of grammarians. It is not necessary after a verb in a sentence that needs only commas. (“I need sugar, flour, and baking powder.”) Capitalize only complete clauses after colons. The examples above include three errors: a comma before a list, an unnecessary colon, and an uncapitalized complete clause after a colon.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>When used improperly, colons can lend a different meaning than intended. “Don’t let worries kill you: Let me help.” As long as you use a colon before a list, before a long quotation, or before part of a sentence that explains what was just stated, you’ll be fine.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use a colon, a semicolon, or a comma in the following sentence? He had three options ----- to run, to swim, or to fly. When you make a statement with a list, your best bet is to use a colon in front of it.</p>

</details>

### 39. The Vocative Comma — Dấu phẩy hô gọi

💡 **Giải thích:** Khi **gọi trực tiếp** ai đó, tên/danh xưng phải tách bằng dấu phẩy. Thiếu dấu phẩy có thể đổi nghĩa hoàn toàn: *I didn't eat Jody* (tôi không ăn thịt Jody) ≠ *I didn't eat, Jody* (Jody à, tôi chưa ăn).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Greetings everybody! | Greetings, everybody! |
| I didn’t eat Jody. | I didn't eat, Jody. |
| My friend you’re the best person for the job. | My friend, you're the best person for the job. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The comma strikes again! In this case, the question is where you place the comma(s?) when you’re addressing a specific person. Hail Caesar?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The vocative comma enables you to direct attention to someone specific. By saying, “Greetings, everybody,” you separate the statement of “greetings” from those addressed. It can appear anywhere in a sentence. (“I didn’t eat, Jody.”) How useful the vocative comma was in that sentence! Without it, I’m left wondering why you’re telling me you didn’t eat our friend, and suddenly I’m afraid.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>The word <em>vocative</em> is of Late Middle English origin by way of Old French, and before that, Latin: <em>vocare</em>, meaning “to call.” The name of this comma makes perfect sense.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Where would you place a comma in this sentence? I expect you to correct your writing mistakes dear student. Beware of any person in a position of authority who refers to you this way, dear student.</p>

</details>

### 40. Pride Capitals — Viết hoa để "nhấn mạnh"

💡 **Giải thích:** Viết hoa tùy tiện để nhấn mạnh từ quan trọng (*pride capitals*) **không theo quy tắc nào** và trông thiếu chuyên nghiệp. Chỉ viết hoa danh từ riêng và đầu câu; muốn nhấn mạnh hãy chọn từ mạnh hơn hoặc dùng in nghiêng.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I gave my Oldest brother a copy of a really Great book. | I gave my oldest brother a copy of a really great book. |
| We must Fight for the right to Read! | We must fight for the right to read! |
| That book made me feel Happiness and Pride and All Good Things. | That book made me feel happiness and pride and all good things. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You might have a friend (or maybe your grandmother) who likes capital letters a little too much, even if they have no idea what a pride capital is. Now you can be specific when you teach them the error of their ways.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Pride capitals have nothing to do with the normal rules of capitalization; they exist simply as a way to emphasize particular words. In the examples, the only words that are appropriately capitalized begin each sentence. Rather than writing “I Hated that movie; it made me cry All Night,” try italicizing instead: “I <em>hated</em> that movie; it made me cry <em>all night</em>.” For those using capitalization arbitrarily, italics won’t help. Instead, consider sticking to the rules.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Over-capitalization seems to run rampant when one has little to say that is genuinely important or interesting. Pride capitals are a way to emphasize without being particularly creative. For example: “I went to a Museum” uses only “went.” Strong verbs are fun! Replace “went” with “meticulously explored,” “rampaged through,” or “danced around inside,” and you won’t need pride capitals to make your point. This is your chance to be creative.</p>

</details>

### 41. Scare Quotes — Ngoặc kép "mỉa mai" (scare quotes)

💡 **Giải thích:** Đặt ngoặc kép quanh một từ thường ngụ ý **mỉa mai hoặc nghi ngờ**: *"fresh" sandwiches* = bánh "tươi" (thực ra không tươi). Đừng dùng ngoặc kép để nhấn mạnh; hãy bỏ chúng đi.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Please come and “try” our “fresh” sandwiches! | Please come and try our fresh sandwiches! |
| I’d like to see him “channel” his inner Aquaman. | I'd like to see him channel his inner Aquaman. |
| We are open “24 hours a day.” | We are open 24 hours a day. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Quotation marks around dialogue and quotations are pretty straightforward. It’s increasingly common, though, to see marks around individual words or phrases. These quotations muddy the waters of sentences and confuse the minds of readers, and can be downright disturbing.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Scare quotes are quotation marks placed around a word or phrase to signal its use in a nonstandard way or to convey irony, skepticism, or disagreement. They’re also known as sneer quotes, which kind of says it all. Anyone accustomed to reading normal prose would (and probably should) pause at the door of a business that uses quotation marks around any of its products, especially “food.” Scare quotes also serve the purpose of sarcasm: The four-hour lecture on punctuation was “interesting.”</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>The Ancient Greeks used a version of scare quotes. And although their usage disappeared for a long time, they’ve had a resurgence starting in the 1990s.</p>

</details>

### 42. Punctuation for Currency — Ký hiệu tiền tệ

💡 **Giải thích:** Ký hiệu **$ đứng trước** số tiền (*$4,000*), ký hiệu **¢ đứng sau** (*90¢*). Không dùng cả dấu thập phân lẫn ¢: *.90¢* = 0,9 xu (chưa tới 1 xu!). 99 xu viết *$0.99* hoặc *99¢*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We are selling pens today for.90¢. | We are selling pens today for 90¢ ($0.90). |
| I paid 4,000$ for my used car. | I paid $4,000 for my used car. |
| 99¢ almost equals a single penny. | $0.99 (99¢) almost equals a whole dollar. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Photographs of terribly mistaken signs from shops all over the country appear online, provoking great laughter and embarrassment when a manager discovers that rather than offering bananas for ten cents each, he is offering bananas for one-tenth of a cent each. What a bargain!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Our main symbols for currency are the dollar symbol (<em>$</em>), the cents symbol (<em>¢</em>), and the decimal point (.). The dollar sign appears before the amount: $15.25. The trouble begins when we deal with amounts of less than a dollar. You may write <em>$0.99</em> or <em>99¢</em>, both of which mean 99 cents. Any sum with a decimal point and a <em>¢</em>, however, signifies less than a penny.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>It helps to be aware of other currencies. Internationally, the US dollar is written as USD20/US$20. The euro (€20) is in use in most of Europe, the pound (£20) is specific to the United Kingdom, and the yen (¥20) is used in Japan. In each case, we speak the number first (twenty) followed by the currency (dollars, euros, pounds, yen, etc.).</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which one of these accurately represents 50 cents? $50 $0.50.50¢ Only the middle one represents 50 cents; the left is 50 dollars, and the right is half of a single cent.</p>

</details>

### 43. Apostrophes for Holidays — Dấu nháy trong tên ngày lễ

💡 **Giải thích:** Chỉ một số ngày lễ có sở hữu cách: **Mother's Day, Father's Day, Valentine's Day, New Year's Day** (số ít, của một người). *Veterans Day* **không** có dấu nháy. *Happy New Year!* (không có 's, vì đó là lời chúc năm mới, không phải ngày).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I got her some flowers for Mothers Day. | I got her some flowers for Mother's Day. |
| Happy New Year’s! | Happy New Year! |
| We celebrate Veteran’s Day on the first Monday in November. | We celebrate Veterans Day on November 11. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We celebrate many nonreligious holidays. Some of these special days include apostrophes, but others do not, and there’s a simple rule to help you remember which is which.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Only Mother’s Day, Father’s Day, Valentine’s Day, and New Year’s Day need an apostrophe because they are possessive; there are holidays in honor of each parent (yours), one Valentine (yours), and one New Year. Veterans Day, also known as Armistice Day, is in honor of all military veterans; it does not belong to anyone, and does not take an apostrophe. The day of the new year is New Year’s Day. To be precise, without the word <em>day</em>, “Happy New Year’s” means “Happy New Year is,” but colloquial use allows for Happy New Year’s.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>The first American Mother’s Day was celebrated in 1908 by Anna Jarvis, who campaigned for it to be a national holiday; it happened in 1911. She noted that it is a singular “Mother’s Day” (not Mothers’ Day). Father’s Day wasn’t official until 1966.</p>

</details>

### 44. Run-On Sentences — Câu chạy dài (run-on sentence)

💡 **Giải thích:** Hai mệnh đề độc lập **dính liền không có dấu câu hay liên từ**. Sửa bằng dấu chấm, dấu chấm phẩy, hoặc **dấu phẩy + liên từ**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I made a pot of coffee I drank it. | I made a pot of coffee, and I drank it. |
| She skipped grammar class she returned home with failing grades. | She skipped grammar class, so she returned home with failing grades. |
| His dog is a good dog he doesn’t know how to punctuate a sentence. | His dog is a good dog; he doesn't know how to punctuate a sentence. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Have you ever known a person who can just keep talking and the subjects shift and change before you ever get a word in it’s time to talk about how not to be tragic with run-on sentences.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>A run-on sentence uses at least two independent clauses that are not connected properly. A run-on sentence needs a little tender loving care, or at least a comma, period, or semicolon. “I made a pot of coffee<em>, and</em> I drank it.” Punctuation is a grand addition to any sentence with two or more independent clauses.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>You have lots of options with run-on sentences. You can break up a run-on sentence with a period or a semicolon. You can also add a comma and a coordinating conjunction, as in “She skipped grammar class, <em>and</em> she returned home with failing grades.” A semicolon and a conjunctive adverb such as <em>however</em> would work as well: “His dog is a good dog; <em>however</em>, he doesn’t know how to punctuate a sentence.”</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>What kind of punctuation would you add to the following run-on sentence? They told me that they sold the house they had lived in it for years. Use a semicolon or a period after the word <em>house</em>.</p>

</details>

### 45. Double Spaces After a Period — Hai dấu cách sau dấu chấm

💡 **Giải thích:** Thói quen gõ **hai dấu cách** sau dấu chấm là từ thời máy đánh chữ. Với máy tính và font hiện đại, chuẩn là **một dấu cách**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Look! This* laptop won’t even let me type two spaces without marking the error. | Look! This laptop won't even let me type two spaces without marking the error. |
| Using two spaces looks *odd. Wide open. Bizarre.* | Using two spaces looks odd. Wide open. Bizarre. |
| Using a single space looks *clean. It’s tidy. Oops*, I did it again! | Using a single space looks clean. It's tidy. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Once upon a time, students who learned how to type on a typewriter—you’ve heard of those, right?—were trained to add two spaces after a period under penalty of death. Not getting a gold star was tragic.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The good people who created modern word-processing software decided that computers would be able to slightly expand the space after a period, to save its users from that extra thumb action. This has been true for decades, now that we have moved from monospace (one character per space) to proportional space (lovely kerning that makes the words fit together nicely). Save yourself unnecessary work and join the twenty-first century: one space after all sentence-ending punctuation!</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Irish and Anglo-Saxon monks developed spacing between words at about the same time that they were singing plain-chant in monasteries: 600 CE and beyond. It made reading much easier, and became the norm by the Renaissance. There was usually an extra space after the period to separate sentences; the use of two spaces wasn’t standard until the 1970s.</p>

</details>

## Chương 3: Dùng từ cho đúng (Word to the Wise)

### 46. Sex vs. Gender — Sex và Gender

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Sex*** = **giới tính sinh học**. ***Gender*** = **giới** theo vai trò xã hội và bản dạng. Thai nhi và ong mật có *sex*, không có *gender* (chưa có vai trò xã hội).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| They had a party to reveal the *gender* of the unborn baby. | They had a party to reveal the sex of the unborn baby. |
| The *sex* of my sister has changed over time. | The gender (identity) of my sister has changed over time. |
| It is pretty easy to determine the *gender* of honeybees. | It is pretty easy to determine the sex of honeybees. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Is it just our cultural awkwardness at using the word <em>sex</em> that makes us stick with the word <em>gender</em> to cover all the bases? Or possibly it’s just a matter of uncertainty that makes us choose one or the other.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Let’s be clear: <em>Sex</em> is exclusively about biology, and <em>gender</em> is about social roles and identity. We are becoming increasingly familiar with individuals who identify as a gender that doesn’t align with the sex that they were assigned at birth. An unborn baby has no gender to reveal, but its sex is either male, female, or occasionally intersex (having the physical characteristics of both). So, yes, we may wish to rethink calling it a “<em>gender</em> reveal party.” And as far as we know, honeybees do not express gender identities, so we’re determining their sex only.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Derived from the Latin word <em>genus</em> (kind, family, type, etc.), the meaning of <em>gender</em> separated from the meaning of <em>sex</em> in the twentieth century. Now generally associated with identity, the social concept of gender has real-life usages and implications. Sex, which is connected etymologically with the word <em>section,</em> is about the actual biological characteristics that divide individuals.</p>

</details>

### 47. Weather vs. Climate — Weather và Climate

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Weather*** = **thời tiết** hôm nay, tuần này (ngắn hạn). ***Climate*** = **khí hậu**, mô hình thời tiết dài hạn của một vùng qua nhiều năm.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Look, it’s snowing! Our *climate* is chilly today. | Look, it's snowing! Our weather is chilly today. |
| The *weather* hasn’t changed for over five hundred years. | The climate hasn't changed for over five hundred years. |
| How is the *climate* where you live this winter? | How is the weather where you live this winter? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>These two words, and their implications for our survival, appear on the news every day. We talk about the <em>weather</em> and <em>climate</em> as if they are the same thing, and this confusion explains why some people dismiss climate change when the weather is cold.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>If you make a snowball and throw it, the recipient will become acutely aware of today’s <em>weather</em> and how it enabled you to fashion a (mostly) harmless weapon in seconds. Changes in the <em>weather</em> are visible out your window, and measurable on your outdoor thermometer. <em>Climate</em> changes over decades and centuries, not days. If you build a house at sea level today, you will need significant insurance because your house may fall victim to global <em>climate</em> change.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Climate</em> refers primarily to a zone of the earth, and is a large-scale characterization of a condition; it used to refer primarily to the angle of the sun in regard to a large region. “How was the <em>climate</em> in Mesopotamia?” refers to general characteristics. If you wanted to know about the <em>weather</em> on a particular day in Mesopotamia, you’d have to go back in time.</p>

</details>

### 48. Like vs. Such As — Like và Such as

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Like*** = **giống như** (so sánh tương đồng). ***Such as*** = **ví dụ như** (liệt kê thành viên trong một nhóm). *Poems such as "On Turning Ten"* (bài đó là một ví dụ), *A guitar is like a lute* (đàn guitar giống đàn lute).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| He enjoys reading poems *like* “On Turning Ten” by Billy Collins. | He enjoys reading poems such as "On Turning Ten" by Billy Collins. |
| A guitar is *such as* a lute. | A guitar is like a lute. |
| They refuse to focus on challenging topics *like* English grammar. | They refuse to focus on challenging topics such as English grammar. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>For such a nice word, <em>like</em> causes a lot of headaches, even aside from its overuse, <em>like</em>, in everyday speech. When you want to provide an example of a category, or offer a comparison, do you use <em>like</em>, or <em>such as</em>?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Do you need an equivalency (<em>like</em>), or an inclusive category (<em>such as</em>)? Lemons are <em>like</em> limes; limes are <em>like</em> grapefruits. Using <em>such as</em>, on the other hand, gives you an inclusive set of categories. I enjoy all types of citrus fruits, <em>such as</em> lemons, limes, and grapefruits. <em>Like</em> is not inclusive; when you say that you love listening to songs <em>like</em> “Singing in the Rain,” you are saying that you <em>don’t</em> actually listen to that song. You just listen to songs <em>like</em> it. If you say that you love listening to songs <em>such as</em> “Singing in the Rain,” suddenly a whole array of catchy songs will appear in the listener’s mind, never to depart.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Many beginning writers throw in a comma after <em>like</em> or <em>such as</em>. “He has been to places like, Newfoundland” and “I lived in countries such as, Indonesia, Germany, and Ecuador” include an unnecessary comma. They were perfect without it! Your sentence is either equivalent or inclusive, and that is all you need to determine.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should the following sentence include <em>like</em> or <em>such as</em> to be correct? Do I like pie?!? I love pies ---------- apple pie, marion-berry pie, and strawberry pie. If you wish to include all pies, and I hope you do, choose <em>such as</em>.</p>

</details>

### 49. Irregardless — Irregardless

💡 **Giải thích:** *Irregardless* là **từ không chuẩn** (tiền tố *ir-* phủ định + hậu tố *-less* phủ định = phủ định kép). Dùng ***regardless*** (bất kể).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Irregardless* of what you want, your parents’ minds are made up. | Regardless of what you want, your parents' minds are made up. |
| I have put my foot down and will use poor grammar, *irregardless*! | I have put my foot down and will use poor grammar, regardless! |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>There is something inherently rebellious about the word <em>regardless</em>. It means ignoring the rules, being feisty, and offering no regard to the way things should be done. Maybe that’s why people want to change it.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The best thing about <em>regardless</em> is its devil-may-care attitude. It implies that one is a free spirit who cares about neither grammar nor propriety. When you push it further by turning a perfectly good word into a double negative—irregardless—you immediately cross over into risky grammatical territory. Stick with <em>regardless</em> and you’ll never be wrong.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Merriam-Webster’s website refers to <em>disirregardlessers</em> as “a small and polite group.” Count yourself among them! You always mean <em>regardless</em>, regardless of how rebellious you wish to appear.</p>

</details>

### 50. Set vs. Sit — Set và Sit

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Sit*** (sit – sat – sat) = **ngồi**, việc **cơ thể bạn** làm, không có tân ngữ. ***Set*** (set – set – set) = **đặt** **vật gì** xuống, luôn có tân ngữ.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Look at him, *setting* there. | Look at him, sitting there. |
| *Sit* that lottery ticket right here. | Set that lottery ticket right here. |
| She couldn’t *set* down fast enough. | She couldn't sit down fast enough. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>As any fan of Scrabble knows, one little letter can make all the difference. Furthering the confusion between <em>set</em> and <em>sit</em> is the fact that they’re often pronounced similarly. It’s time to <em>set</em> things straight—you may need to <em>sit</em> down for this!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Sit</em> is one of the first verbs that children and dogs learn. Because it’s so direct and almost primal in its immediacy, let it be your first choice when your body is involved. When you <em>sit</em> down, your action is complete. <em>Set</em> is for other things: objects, teacups, and lottery tickets. When you <em>set</em> something down, you need an item—a lottery ticket, for example—to place on the table.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Sit</em> is an intransitive verb, which means that it is complete as is, and it doesn’t need an object. <em>Set</em> is transitive, which means it always needs to connect to an object in the sentence to be correct. Transitive verbs transfer action to an object, whether it’s a teacup or a great book.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>sit</em> or <em>set</em> in the following sentence? After playing games on my cell phone for three hours, I finally ---------- it aside. The correct answer is <em>set</em>.</p>

</details>

### 51. Lay vs. Lie — Lay và Lie

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Lie*** (lie – **lay** – lain) = **nằm** (tự cơ thể mình, không có tân ngữ). ***Lay*** (lay – laid – laid) = **đặt, để** vật gì xuống (có tân ngữ). Bẫy: quá khứ của *lie* lại là *lay*!

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Those cats were just *laying* all over the furniture. | Those cats were just lying all over the furniture. |
| You can *lie* that spare change right on the counter. | You can lay that spare change right on the counter. |
| She said she needed to *lay* down for a bit. | She said she needed to lie down for a bit. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Chickens <em>lay</em> eggs, and philosophers <em>lie</em> in bed at night, wondering which came first. The answer to that question won’t help you know which word to use, but this book will.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Much like <em>sit</em> and <em>set</em>, one is done with your body and one is done to an object. You <em>lie</em> on the bed. You <em>lay</em> the pillow on the bed. As you now know, <em>lay</em> is a transitive verb so it needs some kind of object. Present tense is so easy! Those cats are <em>lying</em> on the furniture, and she needs to <em>lie</em> down. First, though, she should <em>lay</em> that spare change on the counter.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>This word pair becomes tricky when you need past tense: I <em>lay</em> [past tense of <em>lie</em> ] on the couch all day, but first I <em>laid</em> [past tense of <em>lay</em> ] the cat on a pillow. I <em>had laid</em> [past participle of <em>lay</em> ] the cat there before, and when she <em>had lain</em> [past participle of <em>lie</em> ] there long enough, she pounced on me.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which word is correct in the following sentence: <em>lay</em> or <em>lie</em>? When I read a social media meme with grammatical errors, I need to ---------- down. You <em>lie</em> down; you don’t <em>lay</em> down, unless you are a very unusual chicken.</p>

</details>

### 52. Unique — Unique

💡 **Giải thích:** *Unique* = **độc nhất vô nhị**, chỉ có một. Không thể "rất độc nhất" (*very/incredibly unique*), và không dùng *unique* cho thứ bình thường. Muốn nói "đặc biệt" dùng *unusual, remarkable, special*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We visited a *unique* beach; it had sand, seaweed, and saltwater. | We visited a beach; it had sand, seaweed, and saltwater (nothing unique). |
| This restaurant had great food; it was quite *unique*. | This restaurant had great food; it was quite unusual. |
| Visiting my mother is an incredibly *unique* experience. | Visiting my mother is a unique experience. / …an incredibly special experience. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We’ve all had that friend who insists that her baby is really, really unique. However, unless there’s something truly exceptional about the child, your friend is probably just exhibiting an especially acute case of Proud Parent Syndrome.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>There is only one Mona Lisa in the world, and one Leaning Tower of Pisa. You are unique; as dear Mister Rogers used to say, “There is no one quite like you.” Something unique cannot be replicated. It is the only one of its kind. A thing or experience cannot be <em>very unique</em>, <em>quite unique,</em> or <em>incredibly unique</em>, because <em>unique</em> is unique. On the other hand, there is nothing unique about a beach that has sand, seaweed, and saltwater.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>The best way to think of unique is to remember its Latin etymology: <em>one</em>. There is only one; it is unique. Even identical twins are unique because their life experiences differ. With that singularity of meaning, suddenly <em>one</em> seems to hold more value. Wouldn’t you value something if it were the only one in the world?</p>

</details>

### 53. Will vs. Shall — Will và Shall

💡 **Giải thích:** *Shall* chủ yếu dùng với **ngôi thứ nhất** (*I/we*) để **đề nghị, gợi ý** (*Shall we dance?*) hoặc **lời hứa trang trọng** (*I shall…*). Với ngôi khác trong văn quy định/pháp lý, *shall* mang nghĩa **bắt buộc**. Câu thường ngày thì dùng *will*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I solemnly swear that I *will* stay away from the drinks tonight. | I solemnly swear that I shall stay away from the drinks tonight. |
| Oooh, this is my favorite song! *Will* we dance? | Oooh, this is my favorite song! Shall we dance? |
| The dog *shall* eat all his food unless you put it away. | The dog will eat all his food unless you put it away. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p><em>Shall</em> sounds rather old-fashioned; telling someone what you <em>shall</em> and <em>shall not</em> do may sound as if you are speaking to a staff member at your castle. It is, however, entirely correct to use <em>shall</em> in certain circumstances.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Sometimes we shy away from verbs that appear only occasionally in spoken English. Because it is primarily limited to the first person, about something in the future, <em>shall</em> sounds odd, even though it’s correct. It isn’t uncommon to hear <em>shall</em> in a question: “<em>Shall</em> we dance?” We tend to replace it (casually) with “I am going to [do X],” or “Could we [do X]?” While still common in England and other parts of the English-speaking world, <em>shall</em> is rapidly disappearing in the United States.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>These two words belong to the category of modal verbs; such verbs express possibility, obligation, permission, and ability. <em>Shall</em> is related to <em>should</em> just as <em>will</em> is related to <em>would</em>: definite vs. maybe. <em>Shall</em> and <em>will</em> indicate that something will happen, whereas <em>should</em> and <em>would</em>, the semi-modals, offer the possibility. Note: You <em>will</em> receive bonus points if you stamp your foot while using <em>shall</em>.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Does <em>shall</em> or <em>will</em> belong in this sentence? Since everyone here persists in using “irregardless,” I ---------- take my drink elsewhere. I <em>shall</em> take my drink elsewhere; they, however, <em>will</em> persist in using “irregardless.”</p>

</details>

### 54. Than vs. Then — Than và Then

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Than*** dùng để **so sánh** (*more than, rather… than*). ***Then*** = **sau đó, rồi** (thứ tự thời gian) hoặc **vậy thì** (*If…, then…*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| First your friend stole my guitar, *than* he played it on live television! | First your friend stole my guitar, then he played it on live television! |
| I would rather be vindicated privately *then* humiliated in public. | I would rather be vindicated privately than humiliated in public. |
| They ate more lunch *then* I did. | They ate more lunch than I did. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Again, one letter can make all the difference (see Set vs. Sit), when it comes to clear communication. Just think about the potentially tragic scenario you’re walking into by claiming you’d rather warm up by the fireside <em>then</em> go out barefoot in the snow.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Then</em> can be used to indicate a sequence (“He stole my guitar, and <em>then</em> played it”), or it can mean therefore (“If I drink a lot of water, <em>then</em> I won’t eat so much”); in addition (“The cake was good, and <em>then</em> there was the frosting”); or at that time (“Come over next week; we’ll have lunch <em>then</em>”). <em>Than</em> is used for comparison (“They ate more <em>than</em> I did”), but can also express preference (“I would rather play music <em>than</em> wash the dishes”).</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Link <em>then</em> with time and <em>than</em> with comparisons, and you’ll never mess them up again. If you use <em>then</em> to indicate a sequence (first A, then B) and <em>than</em> to indicate a comparison (A has more X than B), you’re in rock-solid territory.</p>

</details>

### 55. Less vs. Fewer — Less và Fewer

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Fewer*** + danh từ **đếm được** số nhiều (*fewer chips, fewer mistakes*). ***Less*** + danh từ **không đếm được** (*less water, less light*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I would never ask for *less* potato chips. | I would never ask for fewer potato chips. |
| We need more light in this room, not *fewer*. | We need more light in this room, not less. |
| *Less* grammar mistakes are something to strive for. | Fewer grammar mistakes are something to strive for. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>If you cringe at the sight of a “10 items or less” sign in a grocery store, you are a true grammarian! If you think the sign is correct, read on.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Less</em> is for uncountable quantities such as water, heat, and air. Use <em>fewer</em> for countable items such as groceries, pets, or puzzle pieces. One may drink more or <em>less</em> hot cocoa while eating more or <em>fewer</em> potato chips. We are asked to use <em>less</em> salt, not <em>fewer</em> salt, and to make <em>fewer</em> mistakes, not <em>less</em>. Notice that <em>more</em> is the common oppositional point between <em>less</em> and <em>fewer</em>, and that is where the confusion lies.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>This distinction between <em>less</em> and <em>fewer</em> —in common usage, at least —has minimized over time, but your average grocery store express lane is one of the places where the error stands out most prominently. Just don’t mention it to the clerk, as we’d all rather spend <em>less</em> time in line.</p>

</details>

### 56. Loose vs. Lose — Loose và Lose

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Loose*** /luːs/ (tính từ) = **lỏng, rộng**, thả rông. ***Lose*** /luːz/ (động từ) = **mất, thua**. Người thua là ***loser*** (một chữ o).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| His tie was too *lose*. | His tie was too loose. |
| Time to *loose* that smile, buster; I’m handing back your grammar exam. | Time to lose that smile, buster; I'm handing back your grammar exam. |
| Poor word choice can make you look like a *looser*. | Poor word choice can make you look like a loser. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You don’t have to pull your hair out over this one. Saying the words out loud will guide you to the correct choice.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Letting your dog <em>loose</em> in the park so that it can run with other dogs illustrates the most common use of the word: as an adjective. As a verb, the word <em>loose</em> means “to release something.” When you <em>loose</em> your dog, keep an eye out so that you don’t <em>lose</em> it. When you <em>lose</em> something, whatever you had is no longer there. If you can’t find your dog, you might <em>lose</em> hope.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p><em>Lose</em> (pronounced “looze”) slows you down when you say it; it is as if you’ve lost something. Try it: looooooze. <em>Loose</em> (pronounced “lūce”) is fast! Run free! You know the difference now!</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which word should you use in the following sentence? If I were to enter a grammar contest, the other people would definitely ----------. The correct answer is <em>lose</em>.</p>

</details>

### 57. Literally — Literally

💡 **Giải thích:** *Literally* = **theo nghĩa đen, thực sự đúng như vậy**. Dùng nó trước thành ngữ nghĩa bóng (*sweating bullets*) là **vô lý**: bạn không thực sự đổ mồ hôi ra đạn. Bỏ *literally* đi, hoặc dùng *figuratively*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I was *literally* sweating bullets over that grammar test. | I was sweating bullets over that grammar test. |
| When she saw me with her husband, she was *literally* shooting darts at me. | When she saw me with her husband, she was shooting darts at me (figuratively). |
| My dad *literally* fell apart laughing when he watched *The Tonight Show*. | My dad fell apart laughing when he watched The Tonight Show. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>If you genuinely believe that you’re literally going to die from reading this book, put it down now! I would literally cry my eyes out if you died... or would I?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The best way to know whether or not one should use <em>literally</em> in one’s writing is this: Could it really happen? When you make a statement with <em>literally</em>, you remove all doubt. This thing is actually going to happen. If she <em>literally</em> shoots darts at you when she sees you with her husband, duck! We should probably use <em>literally</em> only as frequently as we use the word <em>unique</em>.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>The Latin root of <em>literal</em> —to the letter—brings with it the assumption of perfect and absolute truth. To do something <em>literally</em> means to distinguish it from a metaphor.</p>

</details>

### 58. Number and Amount — Number và Amount

💡 **Giải thích:** Giống *fewer/less*: ***number of*** + danh từ **đếm được** (*number of children, times*); ***amount of*** + danh từ **không đếm được** (*amount of wine, money*). Hỏi số con dùng *How many…?*

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| What *amount* of children do you have? | How many children do you have? |
| The glass held a surprisingly large *number* of wine. | The glass held a surprisingly large amount of wine. |
| I can’t begin to count the *amount* of times that I tried that card trick and failed. | I can't begin to count the number of times that I tried that card trick and failed. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>In the amount of time it will take you to learn this rule, you could easily create a list that includes any number of correct examples.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Think of this pair of words in the same way you might consider <em>fewer</em> vs. <em>less</em>: One is for countable items (<em>number</em>), and the other is for uncountable items (<em>amount</em>). By now you have read a specific <em>number</em> of entries in this book; what <em>amount</em> of knowledge have you gained?</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The word <em>amount</em> is called a mass noun, meaning that it measures uncountables such as giddiness, water in the ocean, or the northern lights. A person or an item can also “<em>amount</em> to something” (amount = verb), meaning “become equal to.” “My efforts in the kitchen <em>amounted</em> to only eleven cookies after I ate one.” The original <em>number</em> of cookies was twelve, after all.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should the following sentence use <em>amount</em> or <em>number</em>? Hey, I need to borrow some random ---------- of cash. Neither a borrower nor a lender be... regardless of the <em>amount</em>.</p>

</details>

### 59. Could of/Should of/Would of — Could of / Should of / Would of

💡 **Giải thích:** Lỗi do **nghe nhầm**: *could've, should've, would've* (= *could have…*) đọc nghe giống *could of*. Viết đúng luôn là ***could have / should have / would have*** + V3.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I *could of* been a contender. | I could have been a contender. |
| You *would of* loved me if I wrote better. | You would have loved me if I wrote better. |
| He *should of* seen that coming. | He should have seen that coming. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Almost everyone says “could’ve,” “should’ve,” and “would’ve” when they speak casually to one another. It is the lucky few among those speakers who realize what the sound at the end of the words <em>could</em>, <em>should</em>, and <em>would</em> stands for.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The substitution of <em>of</em> in place of <em>have</em> comes directly from the way we speak. When spoken out aloud, “I <em>should’ve</em> attended your party” sounds like “I <em>should of</em> attended your party.” You’re safe if you write these as contractions, but if you spell it in two words, be sure to use <em>have</em>. You should <em>have</em> seen that coming.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The construction <em>should have</em> + the past participle (<em>gone</em>, <em>stayed</em>, <em>said</em>, etc.) and its siblings are all past modal verbs that describe something that did not happen in the past. They all carry a tinge of regret, don’t they? Perhaps we should ask ourselves why we have three different words for something that didn’t happen, for which we feel a little bit bad.</p>

</details>

### 60. A Lot vs. Alot — A lot và Alot

💡 **Giải thích:** "*Alot*" **không phải từ tiếng Anh**. Luôn viết tách: ***a lot***. (Đừng nhầm với *allot* = phân bổ.)

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My professor corrects my grammar *alot*. | My professor corrects my grammar a lot. |
| Wow, there sure are *alot* of errors in this book! | Wow, there sure are a lot of errors in this book! |
| I think about chocolate *alot*, but not so much about caramel. | I think about chocolate a lot, but not so much about caramel. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This mistake shows up all the time. In fact, it shows up quite a lot.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>“Alot” is not a word in the English language, yet many people persist in writing <em>a lot</em> as “alot,” as if it were right and normal to do so. As a unit of measurement, <em>a lot</em> was an object that stood in for a share of something else. When you ask for <em>a lot</em> of chocolate, the assumption is that there is even more to go around. You aren’t eating all the chocolate, but you are eating <em>a lot</em> —an allotment—of it.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Alot isn’t a word. It’s not <em>a lot</em> to remember.</p>

</details>

### 61. Weather vs. Whether vs. Wether — Weather / Whether / Wether

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Weather*** = thời tiết. ***Whether*** = **liệu, có… hay không** (*whether or not*). ***Wether*** = cừu đực đã thiến (hầu như không bao giờ cần dùng).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| He liked her, *wether* or not she liked him. | He liked her, whether or not she liked him. |
| I’m trying to decide *weather* I should go to the store now or later. | I'm trying to decide whether I should go to the store now or later. |
| It’s a good thing they all came in from the awful *whether*. | It's a good thing they all came in from the awful weather. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>These are three radically different concepts that are confused with each other in a startlingly frequent manner. They sound identical, but that’s about all they have in common.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>It’s definition time. <em>Weather</em> is a noun that refers to what is happening outside: rain, sun, wind, and other conditions. <em>Whether</em> is a conjunction that offers just one alternative: whether or not. Finally, although you may not use it frequently, a <em>wether</em> is a castrated goat or ram, just as a <em>steer</em> is a castrated bull and a <em>gelding</em> is a castrated stallion. If you live on a farm, you might look out the window to check on the <em>wether</em>, but it’s more likely that you’re interested in the <em>weather</em>.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Because a <em>wether</em> tends to hang out with the rest of the flock—as opposed to wandering off to look for potential conquests— shepherds often attach a bell to a collar around its neck. If you listen for that bell, you can find the rest of the animals and tell which direction they’re headed. A <em>bellwether</em>, then, is a harbinger of something else.</p>

</details>

### 62. Breath vs. Breathe — Breath và Breathe

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Breath*** /breθ/ (danh từ) = **hơi thở**. ***Breathe*** /briːð/ (động từ) = **thở**. Mẹo: chữ **e** cuối là động từ.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Let go! I need to *breath*! | Let go! I need to breathe! |
| His *breathe* was ragged. | His breath was ragged. |
| You say you don’t want to spread your cold, yet you *breath* all over me?! | You say you don't want to spread your cold, yet you breathe all over me?! |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Some nouns need only an <em>e</em> at the end to become verbs. <em>Breath</em> is one of those, and the sound of the word will lead you to the correct spelling.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>We can easily hear the distinction between these two words. We always pronounce them correctly. However, we don’t appear to see the distinction as we write the words. To take a <em>breath</em> (noun), you need to <em>breathe</em> (verb). If all else fails, read the sentence aloud and you’ll know which spelling you need.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Several other word pairs—<em>cloth</em> and <em>clothe</em>, <em>sooth</em> and <em>soothe</em>, and <em>loath</em> and <em>loathe</em> —follow the same pattern. In each case, the noun (<em>breath</em> or <em>cloth</em>) is pronounced with a strong “th” sound, such as with the word <em>teeth</em>. The verb, however (<em>breathe</em> or <em>clothe</em> or <em>soothe</em>), is pronounced with a soft “the” sound, as in <em>teethe.</em> Do you see what I did there?</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>breath</em> or <em>breathe</em> in the following sentence? She was so allergic to her own feline assistant that she struggled to ----------. She struggled to <em>breathe</em> when she was near her cat, so she had to take medication.</p>

</details>

### 63. Comprise — Comprise

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Comprise*** = **bao gồm** (toàn thể → bộ phận): *The pie **comprises** apples, sugar…* Nói *is comprised of* bị coi là sai trong văn chuẩn; thay bằng ***consists of*** / ***is composed of***.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My apple pie *is comprised of* apples, sugar, sherry, spices, and bay leaf. | My apple pie comprises (consists of) apples, sugar, sherry, spices, and bay leaf. |
| His CD collection ran the gamut from A to B; it *was comprised of* classic rock. | His CD collection ran the gamut from A to B; it consisted of classic rock. |
| The assignment *will be comprised of* research, stalling, writing, and fretting. | The assignment will consist of research, stalling, writing, and fretting. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Many of us long to be considered a bit more educated, erudite, and complex than we actually are. Naturally, we choose fancy-sounding words; just as naturally, we misuse them with abandon and correct others who attempt to set us straight. <em>Comprise</em> is one such word, and it is incumbent upon us to use it properly.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Comprise</em> means “to consist of.” When you write or say <em>comprised of</em>, you have an extra “of” in there. The result is, tragically, “comprised of of.” Instead of writing apple pie is <em>comprised of of</em> apples and other ingredients, simply write apple pie <em>comprises</em> apples and other ingredients. Presto!</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>From Latin, “to include or consist of,” <em>comprise</em> is complete as is. The trick to using it properly is to substitute it for the word <em>include(s)</em>. My students’ essays <em>comprise</em> good ideas and grammatical errors, while my colleagues’ ideas <em>comprise</em> good ideas and the research to back them up.</p>

</details>

### 64. Everyday vs. Every Day — Everyday và Every day

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Everyday*** (một từ) là **tính từ** = **thường ngày, hằng ngày** (*everyday issues*). ***Every day*** (hai từ) là **trạng ngữ** = **mỗi ngày** (*I go every day*). Mẹo: chèn được *single* vào giữa (*every single day*) → viết tách.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I go to the mailbox *everyday*. | I go to the mailbox every day. |
| My *every day* issue is locating my keys. | My everyday issue is locating my keys. |
| My habit is to have cream in my coffee *everyday*. | My habit is to have cream in my coffee every day. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Unlike the tragic “alot,” both words in this pair are real words with different uses. <em>Every day</em> brings new solutions for our <em>everyday</em> problems.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>If you read sections of this book <em>every day</em>, your <em>everyday</em> grammar skills will improve. The first one—<em>every day</em> —is the same as “each and every day” in that the days can be counted, one by one. I take vitamins <em>every day.</em> An <em>everyday</em> occurrence is something that is normal, mundane, and common. My <em>everyday</em> issue is locating my keys. Nothing about an <em>everyday</em> experience is out of the ordinary; it is the usual thing.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The same logic applies for <em>some day</em>, <em>some time,</em> and <em>any time</em>. Note, however, that it doesn’t work for <em>any day</em>, because “anyday” is not something we ever say.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Would you choose <em>everyday</em> or <em>every day</em> for use in the following sentence? I am shocked by the news when I open the newspaper --------- -. Reading the paper is an <em>everyday</em> (normal) experience, but it happens <em>every day</em> (correct answer).</p>

</details>

### 65. There/They’re/Their — There / They're / Their

💡 **Giải thích:** ***There*** = ở đó, hoặc dùng trong *there is/are*. ***They're*** = *they are*. ***Their*** = **của họ** (sở hữu).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I spent the night in *there* kitchen, dancing! | I spent the night in their kitchen, dancing! |
| *They’re* is a huge spider next to the toilet. | There is a huge spider next to the toilet. |
| Did you hear that *their* heading to Australia? | Did you hear that they're heading to Australia? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>There is no excuse for messing up these three words just because they sound the same. <em>Their</em> meanings do not overlap, so just take a moment to think about which one you need. <em>They’re</em> not too difficult to master!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>When it isn’t used to indicate a place (“over there”), at the beginning of a sentence, <em>there</em> is called an <em>existential pronoun</em>. They tend to weaken a sentence (“There is rain”), and they’re best omitted. The apostrophe in <em>they’re</em> indicates a contraction: they are. <em>Their</em> derives from Old Norse, “of them,” which makes sense, since it’s the possessive form of <em>they</em>. “<em>Their</em> parents are so glad they finally married.” Even though they sound alike, separate them in your mind when you write them out.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>This issue belongs within the category of Homophones from Hell. <em>Their</em> and <em>there</em>, in particular, sound identical in much of the English-speaking world; <em>they’re</em> is sometimes closer to “they-r” in pronunciation. Because of the fact that we speak them much more often than we write them, making them distinct in writing is usually the least of our worries.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Which word should you use: <em>they’re</em>, <em>their</em>, or <em>there</em>? ---------- going to set the candy right ---------- in front of ---------- kids! <em>They’re</em> going to decide, right <em>there</em>, to regret <em>their</em> decision.</p>

</details>

### 66. Etc. vs. Ext. — Etc. (không phải ect./ext.)

💡 **Giải thích:** *Etc.* là viết tắt tiếng Latin ***et cetera*** = "và những thứ khác". Viết ***etc.*** (e-t-c), đọc là *et cetera*, không phải *ect.* hay *ext.*

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I like to add cinnamon, nutmeg, *ect.* to my coffee. | I like to add cinnamon, nutmeg, etc. to my coffee. |
| They visited Japan, Thailand, China, *ext.* | They visited Japan, Thailand, China, etc. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Just as they do with the word <em>espresso</em>, people frequently—and incorrectly!—add an <em>x</em> to <em>etc.</em> when they say it. Spelling errors occur just as frequently.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The abbreviation <em>etc.</em> is from Latin, <em>et cetera</em>, which means “and the rest.” When you say it correctly, you actually pronounce the <em>et</em> of <em>et cetera</em>, just as Yul Brynner did in <em>The King and I:</em> “et cetera, et cetera, et cetera!” The problem is that so many people say it incorrectly as “ex cetera,” and those same people write it incorrectly as either <em>ext.</em> or <em>ect.</em> (<em>ext</em>., of course, is an abbreviation of <em>extension</em>).</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Moving from speaking the <em>t</em> of <em>et</em> to the <em>c</em> of <em>cetera</em> is a small challenge because <em>t</em> and <em>c</em> are close together in the mouth; people go straight to <em>ex cetera</em>. The hard <em>c</em> and <em>x</em> are in the back of the throat, so the movement from there to the front of the mouth is easier, but still wrong. Finally, a note on punctuation: The period after <em>etc.</em> serves as the final period in a sentence when it comes at the end.</p>

</details>

### 67. Historic/Historical — Historic và Historical

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Historic*** = **có ý nghĩa lịch sử, quan trọng, đáng ghi nhớ** (*a historic event*). ***Historical*** = **thuộc về quá khứ, liên quan tới lịch sử** (*historical records*, *historical novel*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| The abolition of slavery was a *historical* event. | The abolition of slavery was a historic event. |
| His participation in the Boy Scouts was *historic*. | His participation in the Boy Scouts was historical (part of his past, not history-making). |
| The formation of the European Union was *historical*. | The formation of the European Union was historic. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>If you graduated from college, your graduation is now <em>historical</em>. If you never expected to graduate because you spent your wayward youth wearing lampshades over your head at parties, perhaps that graduation would count as <em>historic</em> for you alone. These words are not used interchangeably.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Historic</em> is used specifically for events that have been important. The San Francisco earthquake of 1906 was <em>historic</em>; the stock market crash of 1929 was also <em>historic</em>. Anything <em>historical</em> has to do with some particular time in the past. That pile of dusty papers in one’s study from 1998 are anything but <em>historic</em>; they are merely <em>historical</em>. No matter how momentous and surprising your high school graduation may have been, it doesn’t count as <em>historic</em>.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>You may hear people use the article <em>an</em> in front of historic (or historical). This is a result of British English speakers’ tendency to drop the initial <em>h</em> in words like <em>hour</em> and <em>honor</em>. In American English, you can use <em>a</em> before historic/historical event, because we pronounce the initial <em>h</em>.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>historic</em> or <em>historical</em> in the following sentence? If Gondwana were ever reunited, that would be a ---------- event. Considering that Gondwana existed 180 million years ago, the possibility of the tectonic plates coming back together would be both <em>historic</em> and quite dramatic!</p>

</details>

### 68. Between vs. Among — Between và Among

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Between*** dùng cho các đối tượng **cụ thể, tách bạch** (thường là 2, nhưng có thể nhiều hơn nếu được nêu rõ). ***Among*** dùng cho **nhóm/đám đông không xác định rõ** từng thành viên.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I ran *between* the crowd in search of my friend. | I ran among (through) the crowd in search of my friend. |
| A sense of unease began to grow *between* the class. | A sense of unease began to grow among the class. |
| He sat *among* his dog and his cat. | He sat between his dog and his cat. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You’re trying to divide your pizza into equal parts to share with people in your office, one of whom is a grammarian. Are you sharing them <em>between</em> or <em>among</em> your colleagues? You know that grammarian will be paying attention.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Between</em> is for specific people (or things, or generic items), and <em>among</em> is for vague numbers. You may use <em>between</em> for many items, as long as they are specific. For example, if you had five teenagers but just one car, you might have to choose <em>between</em> Bob, Ann, Lamont, Jane, and Willoughby to determine who could borrow it first. The car would not be shared <em>among</em> all the teens, but would pass from teen to teen. If you choose <em>between</em> three people for tickets to go hear a favorite musician, jealousy might spread <em>among</em> all your other friends.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The division between these two words conveys a sense of direction as well as an amount. You can move <em>between</em> the dogs on the trail or <em>among</em> the dogs on the trail; in the first instance, you have a sense of forward motion by approaching and dividing a pair. In the second instance, you wander <em>surrounded by</em> all the dogs.</p>

</details>

### 69. That/Which/Who — That / Which / Who

💡 **Giải thích:** ***That*** dùng cho mệnh đề **xác định** (thông tin cần thiết, không có dấu phẩy). ***Which*** dùng cho mệnh đề **không xác định** (thông tin thêm, có dấu phẩy) về vật. ***Who*** dùng cho **người**. Không dùng *who* cho đồ vật.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| This is the man *that* stole my surfboard. | This is the man who stole my surfboard. |
| The surfboard, *who* I bought in Honolulu, was precious to me. | The surfboard, which I bought in Honolulu, was precious to me. |
| It had special markings *which* named me as the owner. | It had special markings that named me as the owner. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This is one of the most common mistakes that editors see, and that writers hesitate over. Although the actual rule might seem confusing, there is an easy way to keep these straight.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>That</em> is for elements <em>that</em> identify a subject, as in “It had special markings <em>that</em> named me as the owner.” <em>Which</em> is for nonessential clauses in sentences (and are often separated from the rest of the sentence by commas): “The surfboard, <em>which</em> I bought in Honolulu, was precious to me.” <em>Who</em> is for people only: “That is the man <em>who</em> stole my surfboard.”</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Clarifying information that helps the sentence make sense is the reason we use <em>that</em>, <em>which</em>, and <em>who</em>. If it’s essential, use <em>that</em>. If it’s nonessential, use <em>which</em>. If you discuss people, use <em>who</em>.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Select <em>that</em>, <em>which</em>, or <em>who</em> to complete the following sentence. I shoved both hands, ---------- I had just washed, right into the mud to locate my ring. The hands, <em>which</em> I had just washed, became even muddier than before I washed them.</p>

</details>

### 70. Its and It’s — Its và It's

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Its*** = **của nó** (sở hữu, **không** có dấu nháy, giống *his, her*). ***It's*** = ***it is*** hoặc ***it has***. Mẹo: thay bằng *it is*; nếu câu vẫn đúng thì dùng *it's*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Look, Mom! *Its* a beautiful sunset tonight for spying on the neighbors. | Look, Mom! It's a beautiful sunset tonight for spying on the neighbors. |
| That horse just flattened *it’s* ears; stop calling it a donkey. | That horse just flattened its ears; stop calling it a donkey. |
| The popcorn popper just blew *it’s* lid. | The popcorn popper just blew its lid. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This is one of the most common slip-ups, and happens occasionally to even the most seasoned of writers. People tend to sneak the apostrophe in for either form, perhaps because <em>it’s</em> occurs more frequently in writing, so it looks weird without one. Or it could be that they learned that every possessive form has an apostrophe.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Its</em> is possessive, now and forever. It cannot be anything other than a possessive construction. <em>It’s</em> always means <em>it is</em> or <em>it has</em>. It cannot be anything other than <em>it</em> + <em>is</em> or <em>it</em> + <em>has</em>. “<em>It is</em> a beautiful sunset, and the popper just blew <em>its</em> lid.” Grammarians will blow their lids if you keep messing this up.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>If you aren’t sure whether you’ve used the right word, substitute <em>it is</em> or <em>it has</em>, and see if either of those work. If so, use the apostrophe. Otherwise, no apostrophe!</p>

</details>

### 71. Whose and Who’s — Whose và Who's

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Whose*** = **của ai** (sở hữu). ***Who's*** = ***who is*** / ***who has***.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *Whose* on first? | Who's on first? |
| I can’t even tell *whose* there because the lights are too low. | I can't even tell who's there because the lights are too low. |
| *Who’s* crab cakes were left in the fridge over the weekend? | Whose crab cakes were left in the fridge over the weekend? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>This tragic error is similar to the <em>its</em> and <em>it’s</em> problem, and luckily for you, the solution is just as simple. Pay attention to the apostrophe and you’ll know which one to choose.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Both <em>whose</em> and <em>who’s</em> have at their root the pronoun <em>who</em>. Whether you intend to say <em>whose</em> or <em>who’s</em> is irrelevant when you speak because they sound the same. However, they must be distinct from one another in writing. As with <em>its</em> and <em>your</em> (two other possessive pronouns), <em>whose</em> never takes an apostrophe. By contrast, <em>who’s</em> always takes an apostrophe to show that it is a contraction of <em>who is</em>.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Would the sentence make sense with <em>who is</em>? If so, use that contraction! “He’s the man <em>who's</em> responsible for throwing a neighborhood party.” If not, you’ll need <em>whose</em>: “He’s the man <em>whose</em> dog ate all the hors d’oeuvres.”</p>

</details>

### 72. Your and You’re — Your và You're

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Your*** = **của bạn** (sở hữu, không có dấu nháy). ***You're*** = ***you are***. Cùng quy luật với *its/it's*, *whose/who's*: **đại từ sở hữu không bao giờ có dấu nháy**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Say, did you bring *you’re* Einstein action figure? | Say, did you bring your Einstein action figure? |
| My friend says that *your* not as funny as you look. | My friend says that you're not as funny as you look. |
| Be careful; *your* about to tip over. | Be careful; you're about to tip over. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The alleged intimacy of social media tempts many of us to make cryptic comments on each other’s posts, such as “LOL <em>your</em> funny.” The person merrily scrolls down to the next post while the rest of us recoil in horror, stammering “my funny WHAT?!?” Meanwhile, the next comment might be “I spotted <em>you’re</em> error, dude!”</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Your</em> is a possessive pronoun, and possessive pronouns do not need an apostrophe. You should notice a pattern here, connected with <em>its</em> and <em>whose</em>. Once you lose <em>your</em> way, your dog may have lost <em>its</em> way as well. Luckily, you can’t lose <em>your</em> apostrophes because you don’t need them. Because <em>you’re</em> always means <em>you are</em>, it has nothing to do with possession. The contraction needs an apostrophe, as they always do, and the possessive pronoun does not.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>This tip is familiar by now: Substitute <em>you are</em> and decide whether it makes sense in the context of the sentence. Correct: <em>You’re</em> (“you are”?) a brilliant and grammatically correct role model for us all! Incorrect: <em>You’re</em> (“you are”?) grammar posts make no sense, so stick with “<em>your</em> grammar posts.”</p>

</details>

### 73. i.e. and e.g. — i.e. và e.g.

💡 **Giải thích:** ***i.e.*** (*id est*) = **tức là, nói cách khác** (giải thích lại cho rõ). ***e.g.*** (*exempli gratia*) = **ví dụ như** (nêu vài ví dụ trong nhiều cái). Mẹo: *e.g.* = **e**xample **g**iven; *i.e.* = **i**n **e**ssence.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My students need help with grammar; *e.g.,* they forget grammar rules. | My students need help with grammar; i.e., they forget grammar rules. |
| The buffet included plenty of options ( *i.e.*, chicken, steak, and fish). | The buffet included plenty of options (e.g., chicken, steak, and fish). |
| The store is closed, *e.g.*, shut down for good. | The store is closed, i.e., shut down for good. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Any time we directly import Latin words (such as <em>et cetera</em>, as we’ve already seen, and <em>ipso facto</em>, <em>per se</em>, or <em>ergo</em>) into our writing, we run the risk of embarrassing ourselves. Even though they’re just two letters each, <em>i.e.</em> and <em>e.g.</em> seem to cause a lot of confusion.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The Latin phrase <em>id est</em>, abbreviated <em>i.e.</em>, means “that is.” The Latin phrase <em>exempli gratia</em>, abbreviated <em>e.g.</em>, means “for example.” When using <em>i.e.</em> and <em>e.g</em>. in a sentence, use a comma or semicolon before and a comma after, as in “David loves to travel; i.e., he has an adventurous spirit.” Note that the last phrase is complete. You may also use parentheses: “Our neighborhood deer love to eat vegetation (<em>e.g.</em>, grass, leaves, twigs).”</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Your best bet in remembering the difference between these two is to think about <em>i.e.</em> as standing for “in essence,” and the <em>e</em> of <em>e.g.</em> as “example.”</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>e.g.</em> or <em>i.e.</em> in the following sentence? My 87-year-old mother understands textspeak, ----------, LOL, BRB, and IKR. Because LOL, BRB, and IKR are <em>examples</em> of textspeak, use <em>e.g.</em></p>

</details>

### 74. A While and Awhile — A while và Awhile

💡 **Giải thích:** ***A while*** (cụm danh từ) = **một lúc**, dùng **sau giới từ** (*for a while, in a while*). ***Awhile*** (trạng từ) = **một lúc** nhưng **không** đi với giới từ (*sit awhile*). Mẹo: có *for/in* → viết tách.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| He has wasted their time *for awhile* and he needs to stop. | He has wasted their time for a while, and he needs to stop. |
| I need you to sit and practice your euphonium *a while*. | I need you to sit and practice your euphonium awhile. |
| I’ll give you your allowance *in awhile* if you are patient. | I'll give you your allowance in a while if you are patient. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Has it been awhile since you thought about how you use <em>awhile</em> and <em>a while</em>? Even if it seems as if you are allowed to use them interchangeably, thou shalt not!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Our internal haze about which one is correct under which circumstances has to do with the fact that they sound exactly alike when we say them out loud. <em>Awhile</em> can appear right after a verb —<em>wait awhile, read awhile, dance awhile</em> —while (ha!) the second, <em>a while</em>, needs a preposition such as <em>for</em>, <em>after</em>, or <em>in</em> to be complete. Let’s think about this <em>for a while</em>. I’ll get to your question <em>in a while. Stay awhile</em> so we can chat <em>for a while</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The grammatical key behind <em>awhile</em> and <em>a while</em> is that: <em>awhile</em> is an adverb, and <em>a while</em> is a noun phrase: a noun (<em>while</em>) + an article (<em>a</em>). Notice that if you use the colloquial “sit here for a spell,” nothing could tempt you to write “for aspell.” You automatically use <em>for</em>, and you separate <em>a</em> and <em>spell</em>: three separate words of “for a spell.” Write <em>for a while</em> the same way.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should the following sentence use <em>awhile</em> or <em>a while</em>? I haven’t heard from you for ----------; did I correct your grammar usage too often? The phrase <em>for a while</em> is correct.</p>

</details>

## Chương 4: Văn phong (High Style)

### 75. Order of Adjectives — Thứ tự tính từ

💡 **Giải thích:** Người bản xứ xếp tính từ theo thứ tự ngầm: **số lượng → ý kiến → kích thước → tuổi/hình dạng → màu sắc → nguồn gốc/chất liệu → mục đích → danh từ** (OSASCOMP).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| A pulsing *shiny bright tiny* piece of glitter is sparkling on your nose. | A tiny, shiny, bright, pulsing piece of glitter is sparkling on your nose. |
| I just bought *handmade brown two acoustic expensive classical beautiful* guitars. | I just bought two beautiful, expensive, brown, handmade classical acoustic guitars. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>From the perspective of our hapless English-learning friends around the globe, the order in which we place our adjectives seems completely arbitrary. How is it that a <em>big red rubber ball</em> could sound and feel so perfect to a native speaker of English, while a <em>rubber red big ball</em> is so comically, tragically wrong?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>As languages evolve over time, they develop certain conventions that serve as the norm for their speakers. Native English speakers often just <em>know</em> when something sounds wrong, even if they may not know that they’re adhering to a rule—in this situation, a rule about the order of adjectives—and they can’t name it. In English, adjectives appear in the following order: quantity (<em>one</em>), quality (<em>good</em>), size (<em>small</em>), age (<em>new</em>), shape (<em>square</em>), color (<em>cobalt</em>), origin (<em>Austrian</em>), material (<em>bamboo</em>), and qualifier (<em>borrowed</em>) plus noun (<em>jewelry box</em>). You have <em>one good small new square cobalt Austrian bamboo borrowed</em> jewelry box. Even if we remove half of these categories, they appear in the same order: It’s a <em>small square bamboo</em> jewelry box.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>If you use more than three or four adjectives, your readers will likely roll their eyes at your purple prose. Of all the stealthy, bizarre rules to innately understand in one’s native language, this has to be one of the most arcane.</p>

</details>

### 76. Further vs. Farther — Further và Farther

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Farther*** = xa hơn về **khoảng cách vật lý** (*far*). ***Further*** = **thêm nữa, sâu hơn** về mức độ, nghĩa bóng (*further explore, further information*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| We need to walk about two miles *further* to get to the ice cream factory. | We need to walk about two miles farther to get to the ice cream factory. |
| You should *farther* explore the wisdom of wearing a clown nose to an interview. | You should further explore the wisdom of wearing a clown nose to an interview. |
| They’ll need to get gas in the car if they want to drive any *further*. | They'll need to get gas in the car if they want to drive any farther. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>When considering <em>further</em> and <em>farther</em>, it is easy to imagine the toiling writer slowing down, thinking carefully, and using the wrong word. Is it <em>farther</em> or <em>further</em>? Should I give up and reframe my sentence?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p><em>Farther</em> is about physical distance. How <em>far</em> can you drive? How much <em>farther</em> can you drive? If you want to walk any <em>farther</em> to get to the ice cream factory, I’m going to hitch a ride. The metaphorical use of “farther down the road” to mean “in the future” retains that sense of distance. <em>Further</em> refers to the idea of going more fully into an issue: examining it <em>further</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Further</em> takes you deep into the weeds of exploration and discovery. If you discuss something <em>further</em>, you ponder its origins, trajectory, and deeper implications. Derived from Old English, it means “to a greater extent” rather than a physical distance traveled. It also functions as a verb: to <em>further</em> one’s goals. Oddly enough, <em>further</em> and <em>farther</em> were not historically separated in meaning.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you end the following sentence with <em>further</em> or <em>farther</em>? I refuse to discuss this grammatical matter any ----------. If you refuse to discuss this matter any <em>further</em>, it means that you no longer wish to explore it in any detail.</p>

</details>

### 77. Accept vs. Except — Accept và Except

💡 **Giải thích:** ***Accept*** (động từ) = **chấp nhận, nhận**. ***Except*** (giới từ) = **ngoại trừ**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Why did my students refuse to *except* my suggestions? | Why did my students refuse to accept my suggestions? |
| I love chocolates, *accept* the ones with cherries inside. | I love chocolates, except the ones with cherries inside. |
| I am honored to *except* the job of being a teacher. | I am honored to accept the job of being a teacher. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>If you drive through a parking lot in an average American city, you’ll see signs pointing to places where you may or may not park. A sign may read, “No Parking Except Employees,” meaning only employees may park there. <em>Accept and except</em> sound similar when spoken aloud, but they have opposite meanings.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>To <em>accept</em> means to receive or acknowledge, while <em>except</em> means to remove, exclude, or disallow. Students refuse to <em>accept</em> (receive) suggestions when they are worded poorly. I really do love chocolate, <em>except</em> (excluding) the ones with cherries; they have a cloying taste, so I won’t eat them. I <em>accepted</em> the job of being a teacher, and it is an honor to do this work.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Both <em>accept</em> (“take something to oneself”) and <em>except</em> (“take out”) are rooted in Latin; notice, however, that <em>accept</em> describes an act of taking in and <em>except</em> describes an act of taking out. Any one of us can <em>accept</em> (take in) a number of criticisms, <em>except</em> (take out) the mean-spirited ones.</p>

</details>

### 78. Redundancies — Lặp từ thừa (redundancy)

💡 **Giải thích:** Đừng nói cùng một ý hai lần: *that point in time* → *that point / then*; *cash money* → *cash*; *quickly ran as fast as* → chọn một. Viết **gọn** để nhấn mạnh điều quan trọng.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I was a concise writer until *that point in time*. | I was a concise writer until then. |
| He paid for that bouncy house with *cash money*. | He paid for that bouncy house with cash. |
| They *quickly* ran as *fast* as they could. | They ran as fast as they could. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We have all clawed our way through the weeds of redundancy: <em>at this point in time</em>, <em>past history</em>, <em>final ending</em>, <em>burning hot fire</em>, and many more. The only thing they do is stall getting to the point. They do not make one’s writing more descriptive, graceful, or elegant.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>It is no surprise that water is wet, for example, so why saddle your prose with that redundancy? Instead, use your words to convey the characteristic of its wetness rather than to repeat that it is wet: Water can ooze, splash, drip, pour, and cascade right off the page in your readers’ minds. You needn’t write “unexpected surprise” because all surprises are unexpected, and “pretenses” don’t need to be identified as “false.” Pay for that bouncy house in cash.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>This type of redundancy is called a <em>pleonasm</em>, which means “the use of more words than necessary.” Common in academia and business alike, pleonasms offer the illusion of clarification without actually clarifying anything. A redundancy simply takes up space rather than adding elegance.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>How can you make this sentence more concise? At that point in time, I trimmed the fat from the beef in preparation for the big feast. I trimmed the beef in preparation for the feast.</p>

</details>

### 79. Clichés — Sáo ngữ (clichés)

💡 **Giải thích:** Thành ngữ dùng quá nhiều (*poor as church mice, at the end of the day…*) khiến bài viết nhạt và thiếu sáng tạo. Hãy nói thẳng ý cốt lõi bằng lời của mình.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I’ll *shuffle off this mortal coil* when I’m good and ready. | I'll die when I'm good and ready. |
| You want a new car? We are *poor as church mice*. | You want a new car? We can't afford it; we're broke. |
| I hope you recognize that *his elevator doesn’t go to the top floor*. | I hope you recognize that he isn't very bright. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Teachers and professors would do a face-palm if the first sentence they encountered in a paper mentioned either <em>as old as the hills</em> or <em>since the dawn of time</em>. A cliché can be mildly entertaining, as in <em>a face only a mother could love</em> or <em>economical with the truth</em>, but don’t overuse them.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Most clichés are folkloric sayings from occupations (<em>all hands on deck</em>), sports (<em>take one for the team</em>), religions (<em>robbing Peter to pay Paul</em>), famous writings such as Shakespearean plays (<em>pure as the driven snow</em>), popular culture (<em>signed, sealed, delivered</em>), and much more. They express a very specific set of values and reflect exclusive insider knowledge, and are impenetrable to long-suffering English-language learners. Clichés comprise a large proportion of some people’s storehouse of phrases; at best, they add color and whimsy to one’s writing, but overuse results in stale and uninteresting prose.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The word <em>cliché</em> refers to a block of text used by printers; when they placed a block of text into place, it clicked: in French, <em>cliché</em>. Because each of these phrases occurs in a block, they can be clicked into place like <em>a moveable feast</em>. Luckily, they can be removed just as easily. To rewrite a cliché, consider getting to the heart of the basic idea, rather than relying on something that has been written too many times already. You have surely heard the expression, <em>to cut the mustard</em>. Why not say that someone is easily able to rise to the challenge? A <em>diamond in the rough</em> is about great potential; if you wish to convey the quality of a diamond, think about the essential nature of that “diamond” and celebrate it.</p>
<p><b>🚫 Sáo ngữ nên tránh</b></p>
<p><em>• all in a day’s work</em> · <em>• all over the map</em> · <em>• as old as the hills</em> · <em>• at the end of the day</em> · <em>• back to square one</em> · <em>• best thing since sliced bread</em> · <em>• between a rock and a hard place</em> · <em>• by the skin of one’s teeth</em> · <em>• cut the mustard</em> · <em>• deer in the headlights</em> · <em>• diamond in the rough</em> · <em>• everything but the kitchen sink</em> · <em>• face only a mother could love</em> · <em>• fit as a fiddle</em> · <em>• flash in the pan</em> · <em>• fly in the ointment</em> · <em>• give the shirt off (one’s) back</em> · <em>• it is what it is</em> · <em>• it’s not over till it’s over</em> · <em>• kick the bucket</em> · <em>• knee high to a grasshopper</em> · <em>• let the cat out of the bag</em> · <em>• only time will tell</em> · <em>• pass with flying colors</em> · <em>• play your cards right</em> · <em>• run it up the flagpole</em> · <em>• since the dawn of time</em> · <em>• snowball’s chance in hell</em> · <em>• take the bull by the horns</em> · <em>• vanished into thin air</em> · <em>• when pigs fly</em> · <em>• the whole enchilada</em> · <em>• the writing is on the wall</em></p>

</details>

### 80. Jargon — Biệt ngữ (jargon)

💡 **Giải thích:** Thuật ngữ nội bộ, từ "buzzword" doanh nghiệp (*shift a paradigm, core competency, actionize*) làm câu **mơ hồ**. Viết lời đơn giản để mọi người hiểu.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Folks, we need to *shift a paradigm* and locate our *core competency*. | Folks, we need to change our approach and focus on what we do best. |
| If we fully understood their *epistemology*, we could *access* their knowledge. | If we understood how they think, we could learn what they know. |
| They need to *up their game* by *actionizing* their accounts. | They need to improve by following up on their accounts. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Jargon is directed toward insiders; it is similar to cliché in that one needs insider knowledge to understand it, and much of it is quite bad. Academics use <em>epistemology</em>, <em>ontology</em>, and <em>alterity</em>; businesses use <em>think outside the box</em> and <em>land and expand</em>; and police officers use <em>code four</em>, <em>perp walk</em>, and <em>copy</em>. And that isn’t all!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Jargon is both a form of insider-based shorthand and a way to avoid a more detailed explanation. It can be in the form of eyebrow-raising imagery (trying to <em>get your ducks in a row</em> or <em>move the needle</em>), and if it’s causing you confusion, it immediately tells you that you are not part of that particular insider group. It also supports the tendency to turn nouns into verbs, such as <em>access</em> and <em>impact</em>. Be daring! Dare to speak more simply, and with a bit of originality. Don’t <em>up your game</em>; strive to do a better job.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>If jargon sounds like birds chattering meaninglessly to you, then you come by that impression honestly. The word is rooted in Old French and means “a chattering of birds.” It went downhill from there—most of us like birds, after all—to mean idle, unintelligible nonsense.</p>

</details>

### 81. Singular “They” — Đại từ "they" số ít

💡 **Giải thích:** Ngày nay, dùng ***they/them/their*** cho **một người không rõ giới tính** (hoặc người dùng đại từ *they*) được chấp nhận rộng rãi, thay cho *he or she* rườm rà hoặc *his* thiên vị nam.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Every student needs to pick up *his or her* essay so *he or she* can correct it. | Every student needs to pick up their essay so they can correct it. |
| This is *his or her* coat; did *he or she* leave already? We should tell *him or her*. | This is their coat; did they leave already? We should tell them. |
| Every time I lecture, at least one person is always on *his* cellphone. | Every time I lecture, at least one person is always on their cellphone. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Native English speakers use the singular <em>they</em> all the time in conversation. For example, you might hear this: “Ooh, someone left a cookie; I hope <em>they</em> don’t mind if I eat it.” Similarly, you might notice sentences such as “Should each professor do <em>their</em> own grading?” This is normal speech, and normal—casual—writing, but is it correct?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>We live in a time when writing “he/she,” “he or she,” and “s/he” has become increasingly awkward and cumbersome. Saying “Did <em>they</em> leave already?” to refer to an unknown person is fine; that’s how we already speak. As we collectively transition toward more gender-neutral writing, dictionaries and grammar guides are slowly adopting this usage. <em>The Chicago Manual of Style</em> recommends avoiding singular <em>they</em> in formal writing, but now offers guidance for its use with someone who doesn’t identify with a gender-specific pronoun. If you are writing for a publication that relies on a specific style guide, investigate that style guide’s advice.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Singular <em>they</em> was already in place by the 1300s; it became “incorrect” only about 600 years later. Similarly, the use of <em>he</em> to refer to every individual, male or female, occurred by the late 1800s and is totally inappropriate. Because we are actively modifying the usage of this pronoun, formal writers can simply pluralize the noun until <em>every</em> grammar guide allows the singular <em>they</em>: Students need to pick up <em>their</em> essays.</p>

</details>

### 82. Differences Between American and British English — Khác biệt Anh-Mỹ (chính tả, từ vựng)

💡 **Giải thích:** Viết cho độc giả Mỹ thì dùng chính tả Mỹ: *-or* (neighbor, behavior, color, labor), *-ize* (civilize), *-er* (theater), *learned*, *jail*. Anh-Anh dùng *-our, -ise, -re, learnt, gaol*. Quan trọng nhất là **nhất quán** một kiểu trong một bài.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| His *neighbour’s behaviour* was *coloured* by his indifferent approach to *labour*. | His neighbor's behavior was colored by his indifferent approach to labor. |
| My British friend sent her adult child to *gaol* in an attempt to *civilise* her. | My British friend sent her adult child to jail in an attempt to civilize her. |
| I certainly *learnt* my lesson when I arrived late to the *theatre*. | I certainly learned my lesson when I arrived late to the theater. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>English and British English Both American English and British English are correct in their own distinct locations, but things fall apart when you place an English person in an American context, or an American person in a British context. Just to tease our British friends, let us assume in this entry that British English is wrong, wrong, wrong in America!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Leaving aside our mutually unintelligible slang words and phrases, the biggest difference between our two nations, “separated by a common language,” is spelling. Between <em>learned/learnt, center/centre</em>, <em>traveled/travelled</em>, <em>civilize/civilise</em>, and others, the differences are minor. Many Americans still use <em>theatre</em> because it seems to look more refined (and they will defend their choice with fervor), but keep in mind that Shakespeare himself purportedly used <em>theater</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>We can blame Noah Webster, a lexicographer and the creator of <em>An American Dictionary of the English Language</em> (1828), for many alterations to British spelling. He wanted to rid English of any “corruptions” from French (which included –re rather than –er). Our collective perception of class differences through the British use of <em>needn’t</em>, <em>shall</em>, and <em>shan’t</em>, and the American use of <em>gotten</em>, <em>pants</em>, and <em>cookies</em> keeps us on our toes.</p>

</details>

### 83. Contranyms — Từ tự trái nghĩa (contranyms)

💡 **Giải thích:** Một số từ có **hai nghĩa trái ngược**: *overlook* = trông xuống / bỏ sót; *screen* = che chắn / chiếu phim; *dust* = phủ bụi / phủi bụi. Dùng hai nghĩa trong cùng một câu gây rối; hãy chọn từ khác cho một trong hai.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I guess when you *overlooked* the garden, you *overlooked* the missin violets. | I guess when you looked out over the garden, you missed the violets. |
| Be careful to *screen* the living room windows before you *screen* th film. | Be careful to put screens on the living room windows before you show the film. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Words with two opposing meanings make no sense, but they exist. How can you possibly convey what you mean when you want someone to “dust the cake”? Should you use a dusting brush over it, potentially messing up the frosting, or should you dust sprinkles all over it, gilding the lily?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Contranyms, also known as Janus words, have two opposite meanings, both of which are equally valid. To <em>dust</em> is “to cover with particles” and “to remove particles.” To <em>sanction</em> is “to support” and “to condemn.” To <em>trim</em> means “to cut something away” and “to decorate with something.” Confusion may reign when you <em>skip</em> while <em>skipping</em> class. Avoid using a contranym twice in the same sentence.</p>
<p><b>🎉 Chuyện thú vị</b></p>
<p>Janus was the guardian of thresholds; a quick image search for him will reveal two faces looking in opposite directions. Connected to the in-between times and places of doorways, changes, and beginnings, Janus is one source of our transitional month January (the other is Juno). Because the name is old, it diversified. It is also connected to janitor, the person who oversees the transition from dirty to clean.</p>

</details>

### 84. Gendered Professions — Gắn giới tính vào nghề nghiệp

💡 **Giải thích:** Nghề nghiệp ngày nay **trung tính về giới**. Không cần thêm *man/lady/woman* trước tên nghề (*woman doctor*), trừ khi giới tính thật sự liên quan tới ý câu.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Wow! I went to class and my teacher is a *man professor*! | Wow! I went to class and my professor is a man! (hoặc chỉ: …my professor is new.) |
| A *lady auto mechanic* worked on my car. | An auto mechanic worked on my car. |
| My daughter is a *woman doctor*. | My daughter is a doctor. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Have you heard of a “lady plumber” or a “woman writer” (or any other profession)? How about “gentleman plumber” or “man writer” (or any other profession)? What’s wrong with this picture?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Job titles and professions are increasingly neutral when it comes to the sex of the person doing the work. The job of a plumber, nurse, pilot, or caregiver can belong to anyone. The error in the examples is in using <em>lady</em>, <em>woman</em>, <em>gentleman</em>, or <em>man</em> rather than <em>female</em> and <em>male</em>. You might have a female mechanic and a male nurse, and no one would think twice. But because gendering professions doesn’t make sense, why not just call them writers, plumbers, nurses, pilots, and caregivers?</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Lady</em>, <em>woman</em>, <em>gentleman</em>, and <em>man</em> are all nouns. In assigning an individual to a profession, the noun of the profession (e.g., <em>plumber</em>) needs an adjective (e.g., <em>female</em>), not a noun (e.g., <em>woman</em>), if one <em>must</em> indicate it. This tendency to gender professions is likely to disappear in the next decade or so.</p>

</details>

### 85. Religions and Their Adherents — Tôn giáo và tín đồ

💡 **Giải thích:** Phân biệt **tên tôn giáo** và **tín đồ**: *Islam → a Muslim*, *Catholicism → a Catholic*, *Hinduism → a Hindu* (tính từ *Hindu*), *Buddhism → a Buddhist*, *Judaism → a Jew* (tính từ *Jewish*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My neighbors are *Islams*; our children go to school together. | My neighbors are Muslims; our children go to school together. |
| I had forgotten that your aunt was a devout follower of *Catholic*. | I had forgotten that your aunt was a devout Catholic. |
| Your surname is Bhattacharya? Isn’t that a *Hinduism* name? | Your surname is Bhattacharya? Isn't that a Hindu name? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Nothing is awkward about religions or those who practice them. The only awkward bit is that those of us who write about them sometimes cannot seem to keep the differences straight between the religion itself (<em>Judaism</em>, for example) and the religious practitioners (<em>Jews</em>).</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Any map of world religions will reveal about a dozen different colors across the globe, representing variants of Buddhism, Hinduism, Christianity, Islam, Judaism, and others. Buddhists practice Buddhism, Hindus practice Hinduism, Christians practice Christianity, Catholics practice Catholicism, Muslims practice Islam, and Jews practice Judaism. Each major religion includes multiple variants as well as individual branches; that is the norm. Our job is to differentiate between the religion and the practitioner. Your neighbors are <em>Muslims</em> who practice <em>Islam</em>. Bhattacharya is a <em>Hindu</em> name.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Although this is not specifically an issue of grammar, it is common to link a religion with both its practitioners and a specific place. It is best to separate them. Not all Arabs are Muslims; Arab Christians and Arab Jews also live in the Arab world. Not all Jews are Israelis, and not all Christians are Middle Easterners. Believers span the globe, and nonbelievers live in places with some of the most devout followers.</p>

</details>

### 86. Writing Numbers — Viết số

💡 **Giải thích:** **Viết bằng chữ** số đứng **đầu câu**. Trong một danh sách, giữ **nhất quán** (cùng chữ hoặc cùng số). Phân số viết bằng chữ có gạch nối, **không** dấu nháy: *three-fourths*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *15* people took selfies last year in dangerous situations. | Fifteen people took selfies last year in dangerous situations. |
| Our colleagues have worked here for *two*, *5*, and *twelve* years, respectively. | Our colleagues have worked here for 2, 5, and 12 years, respectively. (hoặc two, five, and twelve) |
| I like *three fourth’s* of his songs. | I like three-fourths of his songs. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>You may be asking yourself, this book is supposed to be about words, not numbers, right? And yet, as much as some of us may wish to avoid them, we still need to deal with numbers occasionally in writing. And they come with their own set of rules. Because we speak about numbers easily and with confidence—for example, noting three goldfinches at the backyard feeder—it serves our writing well to be able to get those numbers into the appropriate print form.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Always spell out numbers at the beginning of sentences: “<em>Fifteen</em> people took selfies.” When you have a list of numbers, be consistent regardless of how many each one indicates: “for <em>two, five,</em> and <em>twelve</em> years” is consistent. When it comes to writing fractions, hyphenate when spelled out, but do not use an apostrophe: “I like <em>three-fourths</em> of his songs.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Ordinal numbers tell the order of things (<em>first</em>, <em>second</em>, <em>third</em>, etc.), whereas cardinal numbers count: <em>one</em>, <em>two</em>, <em>three</em>, and beyond. Few people actually write out sentences such as “This is the <em>twenty-six thousandth</em> time I have told you.” We might say these words, but we rarely write them. Instead, we usually write sentences such as “I have told you <em>26,000</em> times.”</p>

</details>

### 87. “Verbing” and “Nouning” — Biến danh từ thành động từ

💡 **Giải thích:** Ngôn ngữ luôn thay đổi (*phone, text, email* đã thành động từ), nhưng tự chế động từ từ danh từ (*to glass, to coffee, to lavender*) trong văn viết trang trọng làm người đọc khó chịu. Hãy dùng động từ có sẵn.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| You should *glass* your back porch; that way you can *coffee* out there. | You should enclose your back porch in glass; that way you can drink coffee out there. |
| I promise to *calendar* our meeting next time. | I promise to put our meeting on the calendar next time. |
| I’m going to *lavender* all the folded sheets so they smell good. | I'm going to scent all the folded sheets with lavender so they smell good. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Bill Watterson (the creator of the comic strip <em>Calvin and Hobbes</em>, about a boy and his imaginary friend/stuffed tiger) had Calvin say in 1993 that “verbing weirds language.” “To verb,” in that context, is to turn a noun, such as <em>picture</em>, into a verb: <em>picture</em>. Sometimes it works. Sometimes it doesn’t.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Language is a living thing. We are accustomed to many verbs that once existed solely as nouns: <em>phone</em>, <em>clutter</em>, <em>access</em>, <em>impact</em>, and others. If you use social media, you might <em>friend</em> someone. It is when these alterations are unfamiliar that they become jarring (or funny, sometimes), as in “Don’t forget to pee the dog.” To <em>lavender</em> the sheets is clear enough; it just sounds wrong. And I won’t be <em>coffeeing</em> anytime soon, as much as I love coffee.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The opposite of verbing is nouning, which is when you turn a verb into a noun, with sometimes appalling consequences: “Let me <em>calendar</em> that.” It sounds wrong every time I hear it. The unfortunate truth, however, is that most examples of verbing and nouning are only incorrect until the moment they become normal, as in “Was it a nice <em>drive</em>?” and “<em>Picture</em> our home, right here.” Sigh.</p>

</details>

### 88. Comparatives — So sánh hơn

💡 **Giải thích:** Tính từ **1 âm tiết** → *-er*; tính từ **2 âm tiết tận cùng -y** → *-ier*; tính từ **dài** (2+ âm tiết, phần lớn) → ***more*** + tính từ. Không thêm *-er* vào tính từ dài hay phân từ: *more sedentary, more bored*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| My dog has become *sedentarier* since he turned fifteen. | My dog has become more sedentary since he turned fifteen. |
| My child is *boreder* in school this year than ever before. | My child is more bored in school this year than ever before. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Because irregular comparatives such as <em>better</em>, <em>worse</em>, <em>more</em>, and <em>less</em> are quite common, children learn right away not to say <em>worser</em> and <em>gooder</em>. Any other such wisdom about comparatives slides right out of our minds as soon as we leave grade school, apparently. It is curiouser and curiouser, to quote Lewis Carroll.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>When an adjective has a single syllable, add –r or –er to make it <em>bluer</em>, <em>wetter</em>, <em>brighter</em>, etc. When it ends in –y, change the y to –ier, as in <em>tidier</em>, <em>noisier</em>, and <em>creamier</em>. Depending on the word, you may need to include the word <em>more</em> with the original adjectives (and adverbs): <em>more anxious</em>, <em>more bored, more annoyed</em>, and [adverb] <em>more vividly</em>. With more than two syllables, always use <em>more</em>, as in <em>more slippery</em>.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>A solecism, such as <em>curiouser and curiouser</em>, is a nonstandard expression or a phrase that is not grammatically correct, but which has come into common usage, usually as an entertaining in-crowd reference. Anyone who has been to Hawai’i knows that <em>mo’ bettah</em> is a phrase many Hawaiians use regularly. Sometimes such turns of phrase become normalized when repeatedly written or spoken by a public figure or author—just sayin’.</p>

</details>

### 89. Superlatives — So sánh nhất

💡 **Giải thích:** Tính từ ngắn → *-est*, tận cùng -y → *-iest*, tính từ dài → ***most***. Dạng bất quy tắc: *good → best*, *bad → worst*. Không có *bestest, finerest*. Thành ngữ đúng là ***if worst comes to worst*** (Mỹ) / *if the worst comes to the worst*.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Thank you for giving me the *finerest* send-off that I could hope for. | Thank you for giving me the finest send-off that I could hope for. |
| You guys are the *bestest* buddies ever! | You guys are the best buddies ever! |
| We’ll be friends when *worst comes to worst*. | We'll be friends if worst comes to worst. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Comparatives and superlatives live next door to each other in our minds; that must be why we mess them up so often. It is one easy step from <em>darker</em> to <em>darkest</em> and from <em>more boring</em> to <em>most boring</em>. We run into trouble when we use the wrong attachment, as with a vacuum cleaner.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>You can form most superlatives by adding –st or –est to the original adjective, as in <em>finest</em>, or by adding –iest to an adjective that ends in <em>y</em>, as in <em>happiest</em>. Just don’t do either of those to an irregular word such as <em>good</em> or <em>bad</em>. <em>Bestest</em> is a redundancy because <em>best</em> is already the best! And it’s when <em>worse</em> (comparative) comes to <em>worst</em> (superlative) that conditions are difficult.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Follow the same rules for superlatives as for comparatives when it comes to the number of syllables, as in <em>roundest</em>, <em>liveliest</em>, and <em>most interesting</em> (using <em>most</em> rather than <em>more</em>). Note that you need to double the consonant to create <em>wettest</em> and <em>reddest</em> when the original has a consonant-vowel-consonant spelling, and remove the <em>y</em> to make <em>driest</em> and <em>busiest</em>. Learn these rules and you’ll be the <em>cleverest</em> writer among all your friends.</p>

</details>

### 90. Feel Good vs. Feel Well — Feel good và Feel well

💡 **Giải thích:** *Feel* là **động từ liên kết** → theo sau là **tính từ**: *feel **bad*** (thấy tệ, áy náy), *feel **good*** (thấy vui, ổn). ***Feel well*** = **khỏe mạnh** (về sức khỏe; *well* là tính từ nghĩa "khỏe"). *Feel badly* và *goodly* là sai.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I have dropped my ice cream on the sidewalk and I *feel badly*. | I have dropped my ice cream on the sidewalk and I feel bad. |
| His uncle *feels goodly* today; he had some ice cream too. | His uncle feels good (well) today; he had some ice cream too. |
| You received an A on the exam? Wow, you must *feel well*! | You received an A on the exam? Wow, you must feel good! |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>James Brown sang out, “I feel good! I knew that I would.” He felt happy, as we all did when we heard him. Because both <em>good</em> (adjective, noun, adverb) and <em>well</em> (noun, adjective, adverb) serve multiple functions, consider the use of <em>feel</em> in order to choose correctly.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>If you <em>feel good</em>, like James Brown did, you’re in good spirits and on top of your game (and maybe you had some ice cream, too). If you <em>feel well</em>, you feel healthy. If you <em>feel bad</em>, you are in pain or feel sorry for yourself. If you <em>feel badly</em>, however, that means your neurological system is messed up and that you may have become numb! You can <em>feel bad</em> over dropping your ice cream; <em>feeling badly</em> may have caused you to let go.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p><em>Feel</em> is a linking verb, similar to <em>look</em> and <em>taste</em>. If you write that the “coffee tastes <em>good</em>,” it is the coffee itself that has the good flavor, not the coffee that is capable of tasting! Use an adjective (<em>great</em>, <em>delicious</em>, <em>angry</em>), not an adverb (<em>angrily</em>, <em>beautifully</em>, <em>thrillingly</em>) to describe how you are, feel, taste, look, sound, and smell. James Brown was right! He <em>feels good</em>.</p>

</details>

### 91. Parallel Structure — Cấu trúc song song

💡 **Giải thích:** Các thành phần trong danh sách phải **cùng dạng ngữ pháp**: toàn V-ing, toàn to-V, hoặc toàn mệnh đề.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| They love writing, dancing, and *to read*. | They love writing, dancing, and reading. |
| I told her to focus, *write well*, and to turn in her essay on time. | I told her to focus, to write well, and to turn in her essay on time. |
| Stop doing these things: playing with your phone and *don’t fidget*. | Stop doing these things: playing with your phone and fidgeting. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>People love making lists, particularly when those lists involve either things to do or things not to do. Woody Guthrie’s fine list of “New Years Rulin’s” (1943) included 33 commands (available online for your perusal). Each command appears in the imperative tense, including “save dough, change socks, and love everybody.” His list uses precise parallel construction, unlike those of nearly all the rest of us.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Once you have launched a list, stay consistent. If you love to write, dance, and read, you don’t need <em>to</em> to set off the last one. If you told her <em>to</em> focus, <em>to</em> write well, and <em>to</em> turn in her essay, keep the parallels. Lastly, when we tell someone what to do and what not to do, we shouldn’t mix negatives and positives freely. Oops.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>The reason parallel construction is important has to do with the need to indicate equal importance in a list. If you have ever said “I need <em>cat litter</em>, <em>food</em>, and <em>water</em>, but not in that order,” you recognize that cat litter is fairly far down on the list of things you need to live. If you say “I need <em>food</em>, <em>water</em>, and <em>clothing</em>,” the parallel structure suggests that each one is important.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Is it necessary to add anything to the following sentence? They told us that we should eat, that we should drink, and --- ------- be merry. To achieve parallel construction, <em>that we should</em> needs to be repeated before <em>be merry</em>.</p>

</details>

### 92. Better Than He/Him — Better than he / him

💡 **Giải thích:** Văn trang trọng truyền thống coi *than* là liên từ (*than I [am]*), nhưng trong tiếng Anh hiện đại, nhất là văn nói, *than* thường được coi là giới từ → dùng **tân cách**: *than me, than us, than them* nghe tự nhiên hơn. Cách an toàn: thêm động từ (*than I am*).

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| He is a stronger man *than I*. | He is a stronger man than me. / …than I am. |
| Your cousin Brandon is more famous *than we*. | Your cousin Brandon is more famous than us. / …than we are. |
| Are these people better *than they*? | Are these people better than them? / …than they are? |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>English grammar can be at its worst when certain key words are left out. We are accustomed to questions and answers such as “Did you go to the store?” and “I did,” because <em>did</em> substitutes for the repetition of <em>went to the store</em>. But what does one do with “I am better than she”? She <em>what</em>? Shouldn’t it be <em>her</em>?</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>Brandon is more famous than we <em>what?</em> Brandon is more famous than us. If we treat <em>than</em> as a preposition (such as <em>above</em> or <em>over</em>), then <em>me</em> is the obvious answer. He is stronger <em>than me</em>. If we treat <em>than</em> as a conjunction (such as <em>and</em> or <em>if</em>), then <em>I</em> works best. She writes better <em>than I</em> (do). Note that if you use <em>I</em>, <em>he</em>, <em>she</em>, or other subject pronouns, the rest of the sentence will always be implied.</p>
<p><b>🧠 Mẹo nhớ</b></p>
<p>Ask yourself what is implied by the missing words: Is it the verb? If “She writes more letters to Mom <em>than I</em>,” we assume that she writes more letters to Mom than I do. However, if “She writes more letters to Mom <em>than me</em>,” we know that she writes more to Mom than she writes <em>to me</em>, even without <em>to me</em> in the sentence.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should you use <em>he</em> or <em>him</em> in the following sentence? I’ve spent more time with you than ----------. I’ve spent more time with you than <em>him</em>.</p>

</details>

### 93. Active Voice vs. Passive Voice — Câu chủ động và câu bị động

💡 **Giải thích:** Câu **bị động** che giấu ai làm, thường dài và yếu. Ưu tiên câu **chủ động** (*Someone broke the lamp*) để rõ ràng, mạnh mẽ; chỉ dùng bị động khi người làm không quan trọng hoặc không rõ.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| The lamp *was broken* by someone who definitely was not me. | Someone who definitely was not me broke the lamp. |
| A bold statement *was made* by my cousin’s use of the active voice. | My cousin's use of the active voice made a bold statement. |
| All kinds of new experiments are *being conducted* by scientists. | Scientists are conducting all kinds of new experiments. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>The battle for self-expression in American culture is caught between two competing ideologies: to be straightforward and take responsibility, or to be modest and go along for the good of others. These ideologies are mistakenly ascribed to active and passive voice, respectively.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>When we use active voice, American readers automatically and perhaps mistakenly give us more credit for honesty, clarity, and wisdom. When passive voice is used by us, we’re said to be unconvincing by our readers. When <em>the lamp is broken</em>, we don’t know by whom. Make a bold statement; don’t let a bold statement be made. Lastly, scientists are the ones conducting experiments, and should appear first in the sentence. Scientists <em>are conducting</em> all kinds of new experiments.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>In some languages besides English (such as Indonesian, Swahili, and Japanese), active voice is considered rude, and passive voice is preferred. Appropriate times to use passive voice in English include when you don’t know who did it (“This guitar <em>was built</em> in 1982”), when who did it doesn’t matter (“The trees <em>were taken down</em> in the storm”), or when you focus on the action (“The poem <em>was cited</em> widely”).</p>

</details>

### 94. Number Agreement — Hòa hợp số (chủ ngữ – động từ – đại từ)

💡 **Giải thích:** Động từ hòa hợp với **chủ ngữ thật**, không bị ảnh hưởng bởi cụm chen giữa (*he, with all his accolades, **is***). Đại từ và danh từ đi kèm phải cùng số (*friends… their whole lives*). Trong tiếng Anh Mỹ, danh từ tập hợp như *government* là **số ít**.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| It’s a good thing *he*, with all his many accolades, *are* settling down | It's a good thing he, with all his many accolades, is settling down. |
| Our *friends* have been visiting the Adirondacks their whole *life*. | Our friends have been visiting the Adirondacks their whole lives. |
| The government *are* changing *their* minds daily. | The government is changing its mind daily. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We can all agree that I am, you are, and they are all aware of how most sentences should fit together. However, the place many of us collapse is in connecting a subject and its verb in a longer sentence. <em>We</em>, of the many childhood grammatical errors, still <em>go</em> to school. But we are tempted to write <em>goes</em>.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The first two errors have to do with the position of something of a different number right next to the word that agrees with the subject. “<em>He is</em> settling down.” Don’t be fooled by the plural <em>accolades</em>; they aren’t settling down. “<em>Our friends have</em> done this their whole <em>lives</em>.” Your job is to carefully note the number of the subject—<em>our friends</em>, <em>he</em>, <em>they</em>, <em>we</em>, the <em>neighbors</em> —and link it to the verb or other words.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>American English treats collective entities (government, band, gang) as singular, while the British treat them as plural: “The Catholic Church <em>are centred</em> in the Vatican City.” Beyond that one distinction, however, number agreement means that the subject and the verb must always be linked, no matter how many qualifiers divide them. The <em>bird</em> [single] and all of its many feathers, two feet, and two wings, <em>was</em> lovely.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>Should the following collective entity be considered singular or plural? I am going out to hear a band tonight. That band ---------- fantastic! If you are American, that <em>band is</em> fantastic! If you are British, that <em>band are</em> “not bad.”</p>

</details>

### 95. Orient, Orientate, and Oriental — Orient, Orientate và Oriental

💡 **Giải thích:** Tiếng Anh Mỹ dùng ***orient*** (định hướng); *orientate* là dạng Anh-Anh, nghe rườm. ***The Orient*** và ***Oriental*** (chỉ người châu Á) là **lỗi thời và mang tính rập khuôn/xúc phạm**; nói cụ thể *Asia, Asian, Chinese…*

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I will be ready in a minute; I just need to *orientate* myself. | I will be ready in a minute; I just need to orient myself. |
| We just returned from the most exotic and mysterious visit to *the Orient*! | We just returned from a wonderful visit to East Asia! |
| Confucius was one of most influential *Oriental* people. | Confucius was one of the most influential Chinese philosophers. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>One of these is not like the others! And since language is a living thing, and societies and cultures change, one has fallen into disuse and disfavor.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>If <em>to orient</em> oneself means “learning one’s environment,” <em>to orientate</em> means to do the same thing in the British-speaking world as a back-formation from <em>orientation</em>. They mean the same thing, but each one is “wrong” in the other context. Furthermore, British English differentiates between “Asians” (South Asians) and “Orientals” (East Asians). It is fine to use <em>oriental</em> anywhere in the English-speaking world, but with rugs only. If you wish to use <em>the Orient</em>, use quotation marks to add a hint of irony. Ha! He longs to go to “the Orient”!</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Oriental refers to the rising sun, which is in the East. Using <em>of the Orient</em> assumes a <em>western</em> viewpoint, looking eastward. Asians are not “east” from their perspective; they are at home.</p>
<p><b>❓ Đố vui</b></p>
<p>What is the best usage of orient and oriental, with or without capitalization? I need to ---------- myself so that I can figure out where to place my---------- rug. I need to <em>orient</em> myself; the <em>oriental</em> rug will look gorgeous regardless.</p>

</details>

### 96. Among/Amongst and While/Whilst — Among/Amongst và While/Whilst

💡 **Giải thích:** *Amongst* và *whilst* là dạng **Anh-Anh**, nghe cổ hoặc kiểu cách với người Mỹ. Trong tiếng Anh Mỹ dùng ***among*** và ***while***.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| Would you make me a cup of tea *whilst* you are in the kitchen? | Would you make me a cup of tea while you are in the kitchen? |
| I hope to plant lavender *amongst* the roses in the garden. | I hope to plant lavender among the roses in the garden. |
| Don’t laugh *whilst* eating; you might choke. | Don't laugh while eating; you might choke. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>We are firmly in the thorny territory of the separation of American English from British English here, along with shifts in usage over time.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The examples above are “wrong” to American ears. Instead, make me a cup of tea <em>while</em> you are in the kitchen and plant your lavender <em>among</em> the roses. Americans tend to view <em>amongst</em> and <em>whilst</em> as charming (or fussy, depending on their outlook) hallmarks of British English. Speakers of British English (and some older Americans in the southern mountains) mostly use them interchangeably. Words such as <em>amongst</em>, <em>whilst</em>, and <em>towards</em> are called <em>adverbial genitives</em>; they are genitive-case nouns that function as adverbs. We use <em>once</em>, <em>always</em>, <em>afterwards</em>, <em>hence</em>, and others all the time. They are just as old as <em>thrice</em> and <em>thence</em>, but we use them more often, so they feel normal.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Languages evolve in fits and starts, depending on word usage by powerful people, as well as in literature. If you read British children’s books, the “incorrect” examples of <em>whilst</em> and <em>amongst</em> seem normal. Even among (ha!) speakers of British English, the use of <em>among</em> is far greater than the use of <em>amongst</em>. <em>Amongst</em> appears to be on its way out.</p>

</details>

### 97. Discourse Markers — Từ đệm/dẫn dắt diễn ngôn (discourse markers)

💡 **Giải thích:** Trong văn nói, *And, But, Or, So, Well* mở đầu câu là **từ dẫn dắt**, rất tự nhiên. Trong văn viết, chúng phải **nối logic với câu trước**; nếu không có câu trước liên quan thì bỏ đi.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| *And* I never thought I would see you admit to making a spelling mistake. | I never thought I would see you admit to making a spelling mistake. |
| *But* you never told me that sentence was incorrect. | You never told me that sentence was incorrect. |
| *Or* I could pretend that it never happened. | I could pretend that it never happened. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>So, have you ever wondered whether it’s allowable to start a sentence with a meaningless word such as <em>so</em>, <em>hey</em>, <em>oh</em>, <em>well</em>, or <em>umm</em>? Wonder no more!</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>These words are called <em>discourse markers</em>, and local versions of <em>so</em>, <em>umm</em>, and <em>oh</em> form a part of everyone’s vocabulary, all over the world. The incorrect examples above are <em>not</em> wrong because they are conjunctions; they are wrong because they don’t set apart the following sentence. A discourse marker calls attention to the speaker, prepares the speaker to begin a sentence, and makes the listener look directly at the speaker. In written works, it signifies casual speech, regionalisms, and can substitute for the vocative (direct address) case, as in “<em>Bill</em>, come here.”</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>You know, no single society in the world will ever stop using discourse markers. After all, they serve a powerful social purpose. You see, we would struggle to continue through life without a little preparation at the beginning of each sentence. I mean, as irritating as this paragraph is to read, the irritation comes from reading too many of them in a row. So, did that make sense?</p>

</details>

### 98. Demonstratives — Đại từ chỉ định (this, that, these, those)

💡 **Giải thích:** Khi *this/that/these/those* đứng một mình, người đọc không biết nó chỉ cái gì. Thêm **danh từ cụ thể** đi kèm để rõ nghĩa.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| I keep showing them *this*, and they don’t understand. | I keep showing them this balance sheet, and they don't understand. |
| She couldn’t believe he was going over *that* again. | She couldn't believe he was going over that argument again. |
| It is because of *these* that our work is never done. | It is because of these interruptions that our work is never done. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Writers like to assume that readers can read minds. Would you know what I mean if I were to write “Look at this!” or “Look at that!”? You would not know in advance, which is why you would need to investigate. In writing, <em>this</em> is not enough, because your readers are one step removed from your viewpoint.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>In each case, the words <em>this</em>, <em>that</em>, <em>these</em>, and <em>those</em> need a clarifying noun. “I keep showing them <em>this balance sheet</em>” is quite clear; for all we know, “I keep showing them <em>this</em>” could be in reference to a cup of coffee. If he is “going over <em>that</em> again,” it might be helpful to know that he was asking her, once again, to get married. Be specific, and all will be clear.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>These words are demonstratives; they demonstrate or point something out. When paired with a noun, they become demonstrative adjectives because of their indication of something specific. The difference between “<em>the</em> books,” “<em>these</em> books” (nearby), and “<em>those</em> books” (in the distance) is that with the latter two, we know exactly which books are being discussed.</p>

</details>

### 99. Split Infinitives — Tách động từ nguyên mẫu (split infinitive)

💡 **Giải thích:** Chèn trạng từ giữa *to* và động từ (*to greedily devour*) **không sai ngữ pháp** (*to boldly go*), nhưng nhiều biên tập viên không thích. Thường có thể đặt trạng từ ra sau, hoặc bỏ hẳn bằng động từ mạnh hơn.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| She wished *to greedily devour* all that was left of the cookies. | She wished to devour greedily all that was left of the cookies. |
| We explored the library *to gladly discover* a favorite new book. | We explored the library and gladly discovered a favorite new book. |
| I picked up the stack of photos *to carefully sort* them. | I picked up the stack of photos to sort them carefully. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Any <em>Star Trek</em> fan will instantly recognize the phrase “to boldly go where no one has gone before.” While its usage has settled into the popular consciousness, recognize that <em>boldly</em> splits <em>to</em> and <em>go</em>, and results in a split infinitive.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>According to some editors, adverbs are the enemy of good writing. This choice is up to you, of course; should you include adverbs in your work, less is more. In the case of <em>to greedily devour</em>, <em>devour</em> already implies greed. When one discovers a favorite new book, gladness is already implied. If you must sort photos, sort them carefully; the adverb appears at the end of the sentence.</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>An infinitive is a verb in its unconjugated form; i.e., <em>to think</em>, <em>to do</em>, <em>to explore</em>. We use them in sentences paired with other verbs, such as “I <em>ventured</em> [verb 1] out in the snow <em>to shop</em> [verb 2, infinitive].” When we split that infinitive in two, we place an adverb between the <em>to</em> and the verb, as in “I ventured out in the snow <em>to [brazenly] shop</em>.”</p>

</details>

### 100. Indeterminate Personal Pronoun Usage — Đại từ nhân xưng mơ hồ

💡 **Giải thích:** Đại từ *he, she, it, they* phải **chỉ rõ một danh từ**. Khi phía trước có hai người/vật cùng giống, người đọc không biết *she/he/it* là ai → nhắc lại danh từ cho rõ.

| ❌ Sai | ✅ Sửa |
|---|---|
| The cat watched Marie while she baked; *she* was working hard. | The cat watched Marie while she baked; Marie was working hard. |
| Jack knew how happy his father would be once *he* returned home at last. | Jack knew how happy his father would be once his father returned home at last. |
| I added my phone to the pile of phones; *it* was buzzing with incoming texts. | I added my phone to the pile of phones; my phone was buzzing with incoming texts. |

<details><summary>📖 Nguyên văn sách (tiếng Anh)</summary>

<p>Usage Personal pronouns are so useful; we scatter them through our sentences without a second thought. We use them so often that sometimes we forget to specify which noun the pronoun indicates, so that occasionally we might think we’re referring to a person baking, but the sentence tells us that it’s a cat doing the work.</p>
<p><b>✅ CORRECT</b></p>
<p>The vague designations of personal pronouns—<em>she</em>, <em>he</em>, <em>it</em>, <em>they</em> — require us to focus attention on the main figure in the sentence. The cat may have been working hard, but <em>Marie</em> was doing the baking. If you cannot tell whether Jack or his father was returning home, being more specific will focus attention on Jack’s <em>father</em>. Whether the phone or the pile of phones was buzzing is a mystery!</p>
<p><b>📚 Biết thêm</b></p>
<p>Personal pronouns stand in for nouns. They make sentences flow smoothly, help us avoid repetition, and offer creative possibilities. Consider “Bob knew <em>he</em> would need all his wits about him,” and “Bob knew Bob would need all Bob’s wits about Bob”! By using personal pronouns to indicate the agent or actor in the sentence, you can avoid writing “The dog watched the squirrel; <em>he</em> leaped out of danger.”</p>

</details>
